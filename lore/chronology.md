# Chronology

All numbered dates use **After the First Reckoning (AFR)**. Year 1248 is the present. Dates before the Reckoning are approximate and must remain explicitly uncertain.

| Period | Years | Basin-wide developments |
|---|---:|---|
| Uncounted Wet Age | Before 0 | Settlements discover that funeral observances preserve local undertakings. Rival flood traditions survive. |
| First Reckoning | 0–179 | Witness cities agree to count obligations separately from rulers' reigns. A common year is adopted without a common sovereign. |
| Terrace Centuries | 180–369 | Cistern settlements spread through Keth; burial land becomes a political resource. |
| Reed Compacts | 370–559 | River communities negotiate navigation and seasonal tenancy. Migrants carry incompatible customary law into the delta. |
| Era of Borrowed Names | 560–739 | Witness families lend their names to people excluded from inheritance. Commercial adoption destabilizes older dynasties. |
| Partitioned Waters | 740–919 | Campaigns divide the basin into competing jurisdictions; apparently local treaties have consequences downstream. |
| Repair Centuries | 920–1109 | Towns rebuild canals and revise observances. Memorials conceal responsibility as often as they preserve it. |
| Thin Springs | 1110–1248 | Rain patterns shift, old arrangements become costly, and disputed evidence returns through trade and migration. |

The [entry index](index.md) supplies dated local histories. Chronological ranges are not cultural stages: villages in every period preserve older and newer practices simultaneously.
