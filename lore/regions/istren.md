# Istren

Istren is a rainy northern coast of fishing villages, timber houses, smoke yards, and small monasteries. Roof repair is a continuing trade. Boats connect settlements when paths become mud. Beyond the inhabited coast, the White Reach demands carefully maintained depots, dried food, and realistic return plans.

Its rulers once staged wrestling as evidence of unity and leased forest resin to outside merchants. Those histories remain visible in ordinary dependencies: hulls need sealing, shelters need beams, and crews need recognition that cannot be supplied through a spectacular victory image alone. Seasonal truces protect named work but must be revised when they overlook fishers, night labor, or winter conditions.

Collected songs and precise dance preserve testimony while remaining performances rather than unmediated historical proof. The care wards supply another difficult inheritance: useful institutions survived a patron whose reputation concealed harm. Residents need continuing provision and independent access, not merely a replacement monument.

In 1248, northern exploration competes with less prestigious depot maintenance for funds. Begin with the Cold River histories for the cost of bad preparation, the Locked Wards for institutional custody, or the White Reach logs for a journey whose safe return matters more than the solitary moment of first arrival.
