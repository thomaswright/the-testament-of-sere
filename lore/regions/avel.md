# Avel

Avel’s islands lie off the drowned eastern coast, where low tides expose foundations and competing versions of early treaties. There is no single island people with one unchanged law. Sail crews, fishing households, shrine keepers, immigrants, and charter courts share sea passages while defending different rights.

The islands preserve the Mother of the Kept Breath as a tradition of finite hospitality. Their practical histories are less gentle: patron partitions, an imposed annexation’s distant consequences, rescued settlers excluding earlier hosts, and a sanctuary charter describing inhabited gardens as empty. Recognition of those costs need not erase genuine refuge or useful independence.

Observers compare stars from shaded orchards; musicians translate agreements into several landing tongues; a ball-casting school carries teaching across political boundaries. These practices demonstrate cooperation without proving that everyone has endorsed one territorial account.

The Southern Gate survived its recent siege by reopening water at a neighboring spring’s expense. Its wordless song is meaningful and cannot authenticate unstated charter terms. In 1248, amendment hearings connect performance, care evidence, representation, and compensation. Begin with the borrowed refuge for rescue’s complicated legacy, the unequal stars for inquiry misused as destiny, or the southern bell for remembrance that leaves room for present objections.
