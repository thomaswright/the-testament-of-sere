# Orun

Orun is the low reed country through which the basin reaches its eastern waters. Banks move, salt advances unevenly, and maps often name places no longer above water. Houses use raised floors, shallow craft, woven shade, and channels whose maintenance follows actual current more slowly than legal boundaries change.

Fragrant moth reed, grain, fish, and boat labor sustain its towns. Their prosperity is inseparable from food imported or carried between plots with very different fertility. Reed cultivation can protect a household from one danger and make it dependent upon another. The Green Tithe is the clearest example: rescue became an authentic limited claim surrounded by far larger human exactions.

Ordinary expertise lives in canal crews and kitchens as well as courts. A masked maintenance assembly records speakers’ responsibility while reducing immediate intimidation. A ferry boycott reveals how access can be unequal even when passengers pay equal prices. Blue water stations make care legible without promising free light or limitless clean water.

For travelers, a shallow route is not necessarily safe and an ancestral seal is not necessarily honest. In 1248 the practical question is which finite crossings, repairs, and food commitments can be sustained while wider disputes remain open. Heret Ford is a useful beginning because its military fame and everyday working geography continually contradict one another.
