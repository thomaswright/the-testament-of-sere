# The Uncertain King’s Crossing

*Route · Namar · Historical coverage: 667–1248 AFR*

Three valleys claim the expedition’s route from similar place names. Animal bone deposits support passage through two but cannot establish direction. Guides sell competing commemorative journeys, while scholars warn that no honest reconstruction can settle every stage.

A route is an agreement between people maintaining its difficult portions, as much as a line across terrain. Tolls buy repairs only when their recipients are accountable for the work. A passage described as open may still be unusable for children, loaded animals, or travelers without an accepted witness. Seasonal detours preserve movement while distributing its costs to other households. Pilots and guides keep alternative accounts of distance because time, provisions, and exposure matter more than ceremonial measures. Closing one stretch can therefore revise obligations far beyond its immediate neighborhood.

This belongs to The Feast of the Unawakened Heir. For fifty-three years, King Hevren expanded mountain fortresses using labor bound through inherited household pledges. His numerous descendants occupied competing commands. At a private feast in 667, nine senior relatives died and an injured heir was proclaimed sovereign without awakening. A surviving uncle ordered a winter crossing to secure the southern granaries before rival claimants could respond. Pack herds suffered terribly and the army reached its objective unable to carry food home. A satirist later composed three contradictory succession plays. Court readers preserve only the version flattering their patron; caravan households preserve the versions in which servants, animals, and weather determine the outcome.

In the connected record, [The Sleeping Hand Limit](b006-the-sleeping-hand-limit.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Sleeping Hand Limit](b006-the-sleeping-hand-limit.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: whether the crown’s descendants can release coerced labor claims without first agreeing which predecessor possessed the authority to make them.

The Namar passes join pasture settlements to caravan markets. Pack animals carry nearly everything that cannot be made from local stone, wool, or timber. Snow closes crossings irregularly, and a route that is short on a map may cost more food than a longer sheltered path. Hospices preserve undertakings made by travelers who never returned. Their custodians cannot assume that a visitor belongs to one sovereign or one witness tradition. A workable agreement must specify which season, which spring, and which people it protects, rather than claiming a universal welcome the stores cannot support.
