# The Herald’s Wrestling Yard

*Place · Istren · Historical coverage: 423–1248 AFR*

A raised wooden yard hosted staged defeats of the royal herald. Workers knew which falls were rehearsed, but visiting envoys treated them as proof of vigor. Its surviving beams came from dismantled shelters whose occupants never received the promised replacements.

The site has changed use more than once. Its surviving structures should not be read as a single intact design: one household repairs what another abandoned, and a later occupant can preserve a wall while giving its opening a different purpose. Visitors who search only for the original foundation overlook the people paying for the present roof. Custody depends on continuing access and maintenance, not merely on a persuasive genealogy. Removing an interesting object may erase evidence of a repair that explains why the place remains habitable at all.

This belongs to The King Who Wrestled His Own Herald. King Varn reunited coastal districts after rival graves were desecrated in 423. He celebrated unity through wrestling exhibitions, casting his own herald as a rebellious villain whom the king repeatedly defeated. A promoter absorbed smaller fairs and controlled which local champions could appear. The court also granted an overseas house exclusive rights to gather waterproofing resin from royal forests. When raiders advanced along narrow tracks using light handcarts, defenders burned bridges but overlooked the paths known to resin workers. The royal spectacle continued while ordinary coastal households lost shelter. Present custodians preserve both unity’s genuine benefits and the neglected dependencies that made its claims vulnerable.

In the connected record, [The Joined Fair Circuit](b011-the-joined-fair-circuit.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Concession’s Boat Purpose](b011-the-concession-s-boat-purpose.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: whether forest households can renegotiate the resin concession without forfeiting the boat repairs their coast still needs.

Istren’s fishing villages and timber monasteries occupy a rainy coast where repairing roofs is a continuing expense rather than a finished achievement. Boats make neighboring settlements accessible when inland tracks become mud. Smokehouses, net yards, and communal ovens provide the places where people exchange reliable news. The dead are remembered through finite promises attached to landing water and particular working seasons. A claim on an abandoned beach can become dangerous when erosion moves a new household into its reach. Islanders generally test an alleged manifestation against wind, rot, and tides before paying for an interpreter.
