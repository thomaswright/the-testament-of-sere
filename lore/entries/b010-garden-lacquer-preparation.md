# Garden Lacquer Preparation

*Trade · Talassar · Historical coverage: 758–1248 AFR*

Workers prepare resin in small shaded batches and discard contaminated material. Military buyers demand quantities beyond local fuel and labor. The craft council insists on bounded commissions, rejecting the assumption that prestige or ancestral favor can remove material limits.

The trade depends upon tools, training, reliable materials, and people who perform work its public account barely mentions. A skilled craftsperson can improve a process, yet every batch still requires hands and a season of available materials. Output remains bounded by fuel, animals, weather, and the hands available to repair equipment. Prices carry the history of these constraints into households far from the workshop. A commercial undertaking that survives its maker can preserve useful cooperation or prevent successors from adapting to changed conditions. The present negotiation concerns how to retain knowledge while releasing an obligation nobody can now perform.

This belongs to The Lacquered Beasts of the Portage. After a river dynasty collapsed in 758, rival commanders divided its landings among eighteen allied households. Their agreements failed to account for the portage connecting sheltered waters across a rain-soaked ridge. A sword assessor established a craft settlement there, bringing lacquer workers, gardeners, and puppet builders together. One builder made moving beast models whose incremental gestures demonstrated how loaded animals struggled on the track. Commanders commissioned grand performances of their victories while supplying too little food to real carriers. A southern advance stopped within sight of its destination because its crews and animals could carry no more. The craft records preserve practical limits obscured by the confederation’s succession chronicles.

In the connected record, [The Toll Without the Ridge](b010-the-toll-without-the-ridge.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Burden Inspection Right](b010-the-burden-inspection-right.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: whether portage households can set carrying limits against rulers who inherited toll rights but no credible maintenance duties.

Talassar combines salt marshes, sheltered boat towns, and orchards whose irrigation depends on tidal timing. Merchants trade durable claims alongside perishable cargo, although only an actual witnessed undertaking has force after death. A repaired boat, a borrowed name, and a neglected drain can each determine whether a contract remains practical. Houses that prosper from distant obligations are exposed to local maintenance costs they would prefer to call somebody else’s problem. Seasonal kitchens and landing crews keep accounts that often describe the region more accurately than its ceremonial histories of uninterrupted family success.
