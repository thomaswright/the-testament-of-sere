# The Renamed King’s Seal

*Artifact · Namar · Historical coverage: 1081–1248 AFR*

The old royal name survives beneath a patron’s later inscription. Authentic custody proves political reuse, not harmonious succession. Descendants want the layers visible, preserving the difference between continued trade and consent to the ruler’s killing.

Material inspection places limits on the object’s legend. Repairs, wear, and changes in custody can establish that it was used repeatedly without proving every story attached to it. Its value is relational: somebody can authenticate it, somebody needs it for an observance, and somebody may suffer if it is removed. An expensive replacement would not automatically inherit those relations. Custodians therefore record loans and repairs as carefully as ceremonial appearances. A collector who purchases the object but ignores its attached undertaking may own something that cannot safely be taken home.

This belongs to The League of the Dry Lake. A caravan kingdom prospered beside a lake whose channels gradually withdrew. After an envoy killed its ruler, patrons renamed the realm and redirected trade. Households dispersed across the basin but retained a league opposing inherited labor constraints. In 1081 a reformer formerly employed by an intrusive court joined a popular uprising and was later executed after promised safe passage failed. Traveling musicians changed styles repeatedly, frustrating demands for one authentic league sound. Rival ensembles attracted patron competition that sometimes overshadowed the shared purpose. The dried lake’s ruins remain a place where migration, solidarity, political compromise, and changing cultural practice are preserved without a single uncontested homeland story.

In the connected record, [The Host Rhythm Verse](b023-the-host-rhythm-verse.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Passage Promise Boundary](b023-the-passage-promise-boundary.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: whether the league can protect migrants who lack old lake ancestry without diluting the finite claims its founding households still need recognized.

The Namar passes join pasture settlements to caravan markets. Pack animals carry nearly everything that cannot be made from local stone, wool, or timber. Snow closes crossings irregularly, and a route that is short on a map may cost more food than a longer sheltered path. Hospices preserve undertakings made by travelers who never returned. Their custodians cannot assume that a visitor belongs to one sovereign or one witness tradition. A workable agreement must specify which season, which spring, and which people it protects, rather than claiming a universal welcome the stores cannot support.
