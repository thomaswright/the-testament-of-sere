# The Air Keeper’s Corrections

*Text · Namar · Historical coverage: 472–1248 AFR*

Corrections distinguish observations from explanations favored by patrons. Readers preserve discarded explanations beside successful comparisons. The format makes inquiry’s history usable without presenting every early claim as either inspired truth or evidence of deliberate deception.

Copies disagree in ways that matter. A shortened phrase can remove a condition, and a copied date can imply that a later amendment belonged to the original undertaking. Dry writing preserves evidence but cannot make an oath binding by itself. Readers must distinguish a record of consent from a writer’s assertion that consent occurred. Marginal notes are sometimes the only surviving testimony of people excluded from the formal version. Establishing a trustworthy reading therefore depends on comparing custody, material, and ordinary expenses, as well as elegant arguments about wording.

This belongs to The Breath Beneath the Oracle. The oracle at the pass called itself the basin’s center, although its influence depended on pilgrims and rival rulers accepting the claim. In 472 an alchemist heated mineral salts and observed changes in enclosed flame and breath. Shrine workers connected those observations to air moving beneath the oracle’s floor. A family of reciters defended inherited teaching while admitting that ventilation affected performances. An allied army later claimed the oracle predicted victory on the open plateau. The coalition stopped its enemy’s advance but lost a king and exhausted several partners. Neither practical inquiry nor battlefield survival settled which authority could interpret the shrine, leaving a bounded mystery entangled with real work and political need.

In the connected record, [The Voice Beyond the Clear Vent](b021-the-voice-beyond-the-clear-vent.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Inspection Before Interpretation](b021-the-inspection-before-interpretation.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: whether shrine workers can inspect the underfloor channels without being accused of extinguishing the oracle’s voice.

The Namar passes join pasture settlements to caravan markets. Pack animals carry nearly everything that cannot be made from local stone, wool, or timber. Snow closes crossings irregularly, and a route that is short on a map may cost more food than a longer sheltered path. Hospices preserve undertakings made by travelers who never returned. Their custodians cannot assume that a visitor belongs to one sovereign or one witness tradition. A workable agreement must specify which season, which spring, and which people it protects, rather than claiming a universal welcome the stores cannot support.
