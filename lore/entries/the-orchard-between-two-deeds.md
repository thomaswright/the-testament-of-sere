# The Orchard Between Two Deeds

*Current tenancy · Vey and Talassar · Historical coverage: 1245–1248 AFR*

The orchard stands on a Vey plot with an individual deed and a Talassari nursery agreement interpreted as permanent title by one party and seasonal use by the other. Its trees are ordinary grafted pears and bitter plums. They did not cause the dispute. Water poured according to both authenticated observances, however, appears twice along one channel and disappears from the neighboring well. Workers now irrigate in separated intervals, recording the loss rather than naming one household’s ancestors liars.

The older plot reform granted independent soil rights without a complete gate schedule. The later Soil and Channel Attachment corrected that omission for new deeds, leaving older claimants to negotiate. The nursery agreement brought useful varieties through a harbor already struggling with its own two-bowl purchase. Its keepers knew the danger of incompatible understandings and still accepted a transaction summarized differently in the two copies. Pressure to secure seedlings before winter encouraged speed.

A visiting reader proposed sorting trees by stellar temperament, invoking an island classification made popular centuries earlier. Gardeners rejected the prescription using the documented Orchard Temperament Failure. Rootstock compatibility, mineral water, and pruning provide better practical guidance. The intervention briefly united opposed claimants around evidence neither wanted misused.

Their current proposal grants seasonal nursery access, records actual gate work, and provides finite compensation for seedlings supplied. It leaves permanent title under review. A worker who developed a divided pouring act has been moved to an unrelated spring while readers inspect the undertakings. Her condition is a cost of the overlap, not proof that she caused it or can settle it through personal virtue.

In 1248 the orchard bears a smaller crop because separate watering takes more labor. Both households complain honestly about the expense. They also continue repairing the same wall, since collapse would harm them regardless of which interpretation eventually prevails. The shared tenancy remains possible because it describes usable acts rather than forcing a declaration that one entire ancestry possesses the only true account. The pears taste faintly mineral this season. Nobody has made that fact into a moral verdict.
