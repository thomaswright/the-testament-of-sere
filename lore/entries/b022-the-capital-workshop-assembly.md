# The Capital Workshop Assembly

*Institution · Vey · Historical coverage: 591–1248 AFR*

Builders compare wages and meal costs across contractors. Some skilled members exclude carriers from voting. A second tally campaign challenges that distinction, revealing inherited bargaining hierarchies inside a movement publicly celebrated for collective solidarity.

The institution’s public account emphasizes its founding purpose, while daily accounts reveal exceptions, unpaid work, and compromises with neighbors. Membership does not imply agreement. A clerk, a patron, and a laborer can defend the same arrangement for incompatible reasons. Its authority is strongest where it provides something people actually need and weakest where it claims that need as proof of permanent jurisdiction. Reform requires deciding who will perform the useful work afterward. Simply abolishing the name may leave the obligation in the hands of less accountable successors.

This belongs to The Capital Carried Downriver. Highland households fleeing famine joined beneath a negotiator who secured refuge through protection agreements rather than claiming an empty landscape. In 591 a dynasty moved its court downriver and founded a new capital beside the main grain landing. Its ceremonies announced victory before walls and drains were complete. A performer portraying a workshop organizer helped laborers compare the unequal terms under which they built the city. An appointed governor then joined distant dependencies into one jurisdiction to redirect unofficial trade. The new center gained real commercial strength while straining older refuge agreements. Present descendants occupy a layered street where royal splendor, migrant survival, and ordinary collective bargaining cannot be cleanly separated.

In the connected record, [The First Royal Drain Failure](b022-the-first-royal-drain-failure.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Protection Is Not Succession Clause](b022-the-protection-is-not-succession-clause.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: whether capital households can revise assessments without extinguishing the finite protection agreements under which highland refugees first settled.

Vey households measure wealth partly in dependable access to a public landing. Their markets exchange grain, lamp oil, glazed jars, and legal testimony. A witness can authenticate a transaction but cannot provide the labor needed to fulfill it. Seasonal crews clean the same embankments on which courts announce judgments, and a decision that ignores those crews soon meets an expensive physical limit. Flood deposits preserve small objects while destroying the arrangement that once made their meaning obvious. Recovering an object is therefore easier than establishing what anybody was entitled to do with it.
