# The Smokehouse Roof Marten

*Creature · Istren · Historical coverage: 1007–1248 AFR*

Martens shelter above warm smokehouses and sometimes steal fish. Feuding households blamed enemy familiars for losses. Joint roof inspection located ordinary nests, creating one small cooperation before formal negotiators succeeded in limiting the more consequential human raids.

Reports describe a living creature before they describe a supernatural emblem. It needs food, shelter, and a place to breed, and it reacts to disturbances that witnesses may misunderstand. A keeper who recognizes those needs can often prevent the encounter later remembered as a curse. The animal cannot be made into an obedient instrument of ancestral power. Where an undertaking changes its habitat, the effect is indirect and costly to other inhabitants. Stories that treat its behavior as a moral verdict obscure both ordinary suffering and the human decisions that altered the site.

This belongs to The Days on Which Blades Sleep. Coastal feuds in 1007 threatened harvests more severely than either side’s raids. Kitchens, clerics, and net crews negotiated days on which armed retinues could not enter working grounds. A stage performer made the agreement memorable through a domestic comedy about neighbors sharing a broken oven. A celebrated financial adviser later pooled the truce’s guarantees, making compensation available quickly but transferring risk to villages unable to inspect the accounts. Public biographies credited the performer, adviser, or cleric according to the patron commissioning them. Comparing household evidence revealed collective participation and several preventable exclusions. The truce survived because ordinary crews revised it when celebrated founders failed to anticipate changing needs.

In the connected record, [The Truce Net Path](b009-the-truce-net-path.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Named Work Protection](b009-the-named-work-protection.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: whether the guarantee pool can honor small households’ claims before wealthy patrons withdraw the resources securing them.

Istren’s fishing villages and timber monasteries occupy a rainy coast where repairing roofs is a continuing expense rather than a finished achievement. Boats make neighboring settlements accessible when inland tracks become mud. Smokehouses, net yards, and communal ovens provide the places where people exchange reliable news. The dead are remembered through finite promises attached to landing water and particular working seasons. A claim on an abandoned beach can become dangerous when erosion moves a new household into its reach. Islanders generally test an alleged manifestation against wind, rot, and tides before paying for an interpreter.
