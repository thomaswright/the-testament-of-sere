# The Downstream Fleet Reversal

*Event · Suth · Historical coverage: 779–1248 AFR*

A coalition fleet lost control of a key harbor and trade shifted toward victors’ landings. Valley sponsors lost revenue before negotiators revised budgets. The peace garden’s shortages therefore originated far beyond the speeches and patrols visible within its borders.

The event survives through records made for different purposes. A household remembers hunger; a court records jurisdiction; a custodian records the interruption of an observance. Their dates can be reconciled without making their explanations identical. Later commemorations often select a decisive moment because a ceremony needs a beginning and an end. The consequences were less orderly, traveling through marriages, prices, and people who moved away. An honest account follows those consequences far enough to distinguish a temporary outcome from an agreement that still constrains the present.

This belongs to The Epic and the Unoccupied Garden. An upland founder’s life survived through performed epic alongside sparse independent records. Later households used the epic to justify incompatible claims to a fertile valley. In 779 a negotiator established a garden zone from which armed officers withdrew for talks, hoping protected meeting space would end recurring conflict. Outside coalitions continued contesting its borders and access. A naval battle far downstream redirected trade, weakening sponsors who had supplied the negotiations and strengthening new patrons. The peace zone became both a genuine refuge and a place where rivals accumulated influence. Present gardeners compare heroic memory, signed terms, and changed supply routes to preserve talks without pretending withdrawal of one force made the valley free of coercion.

In the connected record, [The Founder’s Unverified Saddle](b039-the-founder-s-unverified-saddle.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Resident Within Neutrality](b039-the-resident-within-neutrality.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: whether the meeting garden can remain protected when the trade revenues supporting its food and repairs now pass through rival downstream hands.

The Suth valleys cultivate grafted orchards and irrigated gardens amid dry hills. Food production depends on precise local knowledge: when to open a channel, which rootstock tolerates mineral water, and how many hands a harvest requires. Asylum customs let strangers work under temporary names, but temporary recognition does not automatically convey inheritance. The same gardens can preserve an old promise and expose its injustice. A court that assigns water without accounting for the labor of keeping it clean may issue a valid judgment that no household can afford to obey.
