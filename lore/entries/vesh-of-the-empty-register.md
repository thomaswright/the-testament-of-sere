# Vesh of the Empty Register

*Person · Vey · Historical coverage: 1191–1248 AFR*

Vesh earns a modest living tracing the names omitted from rental rolls. Clients usually want an ancestor who can establish a claim to a courtyard, fishing berth, or burial niche. Vesh instead begins with the apparently useless entries: a household that paid for three beds but recorded two occupants, a delivery of bread to a house officially empty, a funeral paid for by somebody described elsewhere as already dead. His empty register contains the spaces where those facts should have been reconciled.

The method began with his father's room. After the father's death in 1214, Vesh found two sets of keys wrapped in the same cloth. One fitted their home. The other fitted a room in the House of Missing Tenants, whose wartime occupants had never been entered on the public rolls. Vesh had been told his father worked as a copyist. Payments in the room's plaster revealed that he had also sold temporary identities to people fleeing labor claims. Some purchasers escaped. Others were delivered to the Salt Heirs when their money ran out.

Vesh refuses both a simple accusation and a consoling account of secret resistance. He publishes each transaction with the circumstances he can establish, leaving readers to judge motives he cannot recover. This has made him unpopular with the families of victims and beneficiaries alike. It has also made his work useful in court, where a narrow fact can survive scrutiny that a grand explanation cannot.

His most dangerous discovery concerns the Unannounced War: several officers honored as liberators appear in the rent accounts of monopoly safe houses. The payments might show treachery, ransom, or an effort to protect relatives. Vesh will not choose before finding the second half of the account. A spring under his office has begun forming condensation on blank pages, but he does not treat the shapes as testimony. He pays a mason to investigate the wall. That caution costs him clients who want an oracle and protects clients who need evidence.
