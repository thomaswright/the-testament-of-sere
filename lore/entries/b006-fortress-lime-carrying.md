# Fortress Lime Carrying

*Trade · Namar · Historical coverage: 667–1248 AFR*

Workers carry lime uphill in sealed skins, then return with grain rather than empty loads. Royal contracts require both trips but pay for one. Their petition uses actual burden measurements to expose how a ceremonial labor pledge became continual extraction.

The trade depends upon tools, training, reliable materials, and people who perform work its public account barely mentions. A skilled craftsperson can improve a process, yet every batch still requires hands and a season of available materials. Output remains bounded by fuel, animals, weather, and the hands available to repair equipment. Prices carry the history of these constraints into households far from the workshop. A commercial undertaking that survives its maker can preserve useful cooperation or prevent successors from adapting to changed conditions. The present negotiation concerns how to retain knowledge while releasing an obligation nobody can now perform.

This belongs to The Feast of the Unawakened Heir. For fifty-three years, King Hevren expanded mountain fortresses using labor bound through inherited household pledges. His numerous descendants occupied competing commands. At a private feast in 667, nine senior relatives died and an injured heir was proclaimed sovereign without awakening. A surviving uncle ordered a winter crossing to secure the southern granaries before rival claimants could respond. Pack herds suffered terribly and the army reached its objective unable to carry food home. A satirist later composed three contradictory succession plays. Court readers preserve only the version flattering their patron; caravan households preserve the versions in which servants, animals, and weather determine the outcome.

In the connected record, [The Unrecognized Children’s Release](b006-the-unrecognized-children-s-release.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Sleeping Hand Limit](b006-the-sleeping-hand-limit.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: whether the crown’s descendants can release coerced labor claims without first agreeing which predecessor possessed the authority to make them.

The Namar passes join pasture settlements to caravan markets. Pack animals carry nearly everything that cannot be made from local stone, wool, or timber. Snow closes crossings irregularly, and a route that is short on a map may cost more food than a longer sheltered path. Hospices preserve undertakings made by travelers who never returned. Their custodians cannot assume that a visitor belongs to one sovereign or one witness tradition. A workable agreement must specify which season, which spring, and which people it protects, rather than claiming a universal welcome the stores cannot support.
