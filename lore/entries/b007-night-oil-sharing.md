# Night Oil Sharing

*Trade · Avel · Historical coverage: 914–1248 AFR*

Observers pool lamp oil but shade every light during comparisons. Their finite supply limits meetings in winter. Patrons offer endless symbolic devotion and little oil, prompting a plain subscription ledger that values practical contributions over ceremonial pledges.

The trade depends upon tools, training, reliable materials, and people who perform work its public account barely mentions. A skilled craftsperson can improve a process, yet every batch still requires hands and a season of available materials. Output remains bounded by fuel, animals, weather, and the hands available to repair equipment. Prices carry the history of these constraints into households far from the workshop. A commercial undertaking that survives its maker can preserve useful cooperation or prevent successors from adapting to changed conditions. The present negotiation concerns how to retain knowledge while releasing an obligation nobody can now perform.

This belongs to The Houses of Unequal Stars. In 914, island reformers built a community where work rotated according to declared inclination rather than inherited household status. An observer arranged stars by color and apparent brightness, discovering that similar lights could stand at very different distances. Enthusiasts assigned household tasks according to the star table, converting a warning against superficial resemblance into a system of fixed personal destiny. Mainland patrons supplied scribes and introduced a prestigious administrative language distinct from everyday speech. A peace conference divided the islands temporarily between rival custodians. The promised review never occurred, and the community’s experiments became competing traditions on opposite sides of a supposedly provisional line.

In the connected record, [The Border’s Missing Review](b007-the-border-s-missing-review.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Seasonal Partition Term](b007-the-seasonal-partition-term.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: whether a common work calendar can cross the old partition without restoring either the star castes or the patrons’ exclusive scribal authority.

Avel’s islands preserve competing versions of the basin’s earliest treaties. Sea crossings depend on sails, weather, and provisions, giving a copied text little authority unless somebody can carry its terms home and explain them. Fishing households share beaches with custodians of drowned foundations. Low water reveals masonry that changes both navigation and historical argument. An island’s isolation can shelter dissent and conceal abuse; its effects depend upon who controls the boats. Agreements are usually bounded by named landing places, because a promise to protect an entire sea would have no workable observance.
