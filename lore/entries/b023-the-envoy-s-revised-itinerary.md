# The Envoy’s Revised Itinerary

*Text · Namar · Historical coverage: 1081–1248 AFR*

An itinerary copied after the ruler’s killing omits a long meeting before it. Earlier lodging bills restore the interval. The evidence establishes preparation but leaves private motives uncertain, preventing a persuasive chronology from being mistaken for complete access to intention.

Copies disagree in ways that matter. A shortened phrase can remove a condition, and a copied date can imply that a later amendment belonged to the original undertaking. Dry writing preserves evidence but cannot make an oath binding by itself. Readers must distinguish a record of consent from a writer’s assertion that consent occurred. Marginal notes are sometimes the only surviving testimony of people excluded from the formal version. Establishing a trustworthy reading therefore depends on comparing custody, material, and ordinary expenses, as well as elegant arguments about wording.

This belongs to The League of the Dry Lake. A caravan kingdom prospered beside a lake whose channels gradually withdrew. After an envoy killed its ruler, patrons renamed the realm and redirected trade. Households dispersed across the basin but retained a league opposing inherited labor constraints. In 1081 a reformer formerly employed by an intrusive court joined a popular uprising and was later executed after promised safe passage failed. Traveling musicians changed styles repeatedly, frustrating demands for one authentic league sound. Rival ensembles attracted patron competition that sometimes overshadowed the shared purpose. The dried lake’s ruins remain a place where migration, solidarity, political compromise, and changing cultural practice are preserved without a single uncontested homeland story.

In the connected record, [The Harbor Sounds in Sand](b023-the-harbor-sounds-in-sand.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Passage Promise Boundary](b023-the-passage-promise-boundary.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: whether the league can protect migrants who lack old lake ancestry without diluting the finite claims its founding households still need recognized.

The Namar passes join pasture settlements to caravan markets. Pack animals carry nearly everything that cannot be made from local stone, wool, or timber. Snow closes crossings irregularly, and a route that is short on a map may cost more food than a longer sheltered path. Hospices preserve undertakings made by travelers who never returned. Their custodians cannot assume that a visitor belongs to one sovereign or one witness tradition. A workable agreement must specify which season, which spring, and which people it protects, rather than claiming a universal welcome the stores cannot support.
