# The First Purified Dictionary

*Event · Suth · Historical coverage: 631–1248 AFR*

Editors removed mixed words and regional forms to strengthen a public identity. Readers then struggled to describe ordinary tools. Revised editions restore the omissions, treating practical speech as stronger evidence than the attractive appearance of linguistic purity.

The event survives through records made for different purposes. A household remembers hunger; a court records jurisdiction; a custodian records the interruption of an observance. Their dates can be reconciled without making their explanations identical. Later commemorations often select a decisive moment because a ceremony needs a beginning and an end. The consequences were less orderly, traveling through marriages, prices, and people who moved away. An honest account follows those consequences far enough to distinguish a temporary outcome from an agreement that still constrains the present.

This belongs to The Women Who Named the Hidden Country. Garden migrants carried a coastal place name inland until it became the name of several distinct settlements. Later patrons suppressed their administrative institutions but could not erase household speech. In 631 a materials negotiator organized scarce timber and metal during war, then sought agreements with former enemies. He was murdered amid accusations about his outsider ancestry. Women gathering in garden parlors wrote elaborate wonder tales whose hidden countries unsettled the courts’ official map of belonging. A language revival collected their work beside older oral accounts, sometimes mistaking polished literary invention for unchanged folklore. Present readers preserve both forms and question which histories were chosen to make a people appear uniform after migration.

In the connected record, [The Shadow Tale Door Latch](b031-the-shadow-tale-door-latch.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Speech Without Descent Test](b031-the-speech-without-descent-test.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: whether revived language institutions can welcome their actual mixed descendants without replacing old exclusion with a newly polished ancestry test.

The Suth valleys cultivate grafted orchards and irrigated gardens amid dry hills. Food production depends on precise local knowledge: when to open a channel, which rootstock tolerates mineral water, and how many hands a harvest requires. Asylum customs let strangers work under temporary names, but temporary recognition does not automatically convey inheritance. The same gardens can preserve an old promise and expose its injustice. A court that assigns water without accounting for the labor of keeping it clean may issue a valid judgment that no household can afford to obey.
