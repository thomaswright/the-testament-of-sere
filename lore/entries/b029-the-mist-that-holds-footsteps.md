# The Mist That Holds Footsteps

*Phenomenon · Orun · Historical coverage: 873–1248 AFR*

Footsteps persist over one lake shallows after a refuge promise is broken. Mud gas explains some bubbles and sounds. Pilots avoid interpreting each recurrence as a named casualty, recording conditions while investigating the actual unresolved undertaking.

Witnesses distinguish an observed effect from their explanation for it. Repetition under comparable conditions is valuable evidence; a striking story told after its alleged cause becomes famous is weaker evidence. Masons, gardeners, and boat crews often recognize patterns an interpreter overlooks. Their explanation may reduce the immediate danger without settling whether an undertaking contributed to it. The basin has no institution capable of issuing a final answer for every manifestation. Communities record the conditions under which the effect occurs, then negotiate a response whose costs they can actually sustain.

This belongs to The Diary of the Fog Muster. A provincial commander pursued raiders into a narrow lakeside road during morning mist, convinced that delay would damage his reputation. His force was trapped between water and hidden slopes. Survivors returned to towns where a separate levy attempt had triggered running confrontations along several roads. A household diarist recorded provisions, embarrassment, conflicting rumors, and the gap between expected courage and actual fear. A comic performer later adapted the diary’s domestic quarrels. Authorities preferred a grand account of one awakening people, but the small records reveal uneven preparation and changing choices. They also preserve meals, messages, and repairs that enabled resistance more reliably than the flattering language of destiny.

In the connected record, [Roadside Soup Provisioning](b029-roadside-soup-provisioning.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Levy’s Stated Purpose](b029-the-levy-s-stated-purpose.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: how commemorations can honor real resistance without using its heroic image to impose another compulsory levy on the households that sustained it.

The Orun channels shift across a landscape whose legal descriptions often name vanished banks. Fishers, reed workers, and gardeners share access without sharing a single account of ownership. Transport depends on shallow boats and pilots who read changes in sediment. Salt reaches some plots well before neighboring plots, making an apparently equal assessment unfair in practice. An ancestral claim can survive a change in the channel, but its observance may become impossible at the old site. Communities must decide whether to move the observance, compensate its claimants, or leave the affected water unused.
