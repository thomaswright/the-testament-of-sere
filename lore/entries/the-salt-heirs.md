# The Salt Heirs

*Faction · Talassar · Historical coverage: 612–1248 AFR*

The Salt Heirs are the recognized descendants of the merchants who financed Edrin's survival. They are not a single family and do not agree about their inheritance. Membership follows custody of authenticated portions of the original grain tallies. Marriage, adoption, debt settlement, and theft have spread those fragments among eighty-seven households. Several members are themselves indebted reed cutters. The faction's wealth is concentrated in eleven counting houses, but its public legitimacy depends on the poorer members attending ancestral ceremonies.

Each tally entitles its custodian to a share of the original Green Tithe. The counting houses claim that the fragments also confer authority over every later undertaking issued by the rescued settlement. Their courts have repeated this assertion often enough that many workers regard it as a fact of nature. The wood does not support the claim: different fragments warm in the hand when the ancient tenth is withheld, but remain inert when a household refuses an added cutting day.

Reformers within the faction want to distinguish income from jurisdiction. They would preserve the original shares while surrendering control of employment, travel, and apprenticeship. Opponents warn that releasing those claims would make the maintenance of the reed canals impossible. Both sides can produce honest accounts showing the canals' expense. The disagreement concerns who bears that expense and whether a worker can refuse a contract without losing access to water.

Vesh's research threatens a different privilege. Several prestigious tallies were acquired from people who disappeared during the Unannounced War, and the Heirs' Drowned Room contains records of those transfers. Opening it would expose old crimes without necessarily resolving present ownership. A count house that bought a stolen fragment in good faith still receives grain from families harmed by its theft. In 1248, younger heirs have begun meeting those families outside the official ceremonies. Their conversations are slow and awkward. Unlike the faction's grand origin festival, they may eventually produce an agreement somebody can actually carry out.
