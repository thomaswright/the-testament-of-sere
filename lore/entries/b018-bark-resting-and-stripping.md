# Bark Resting and Stripping

*Trade · Talassar · Historical coverage: 1206–1248 AFR*

Gatherers alternate worked trees and preserve recovery seasons. Output cannot expand simply because markets revive. Their agreements tie delivery to inspected groves, resisting old contracts that treated future harvests as quantities assured by political favor.

The trade depends upon tools, training, reliable materials, and people who perform work its public account barely mentions. A skilled craftsperson can improve a process, yet every batch still requires hands and a season of available materials. Output remains bounded by fuel, animals, weather, and the hands available to repair equipment. Prices carry the history of these constraints into households far from the workshop. A commercial undertaking that survives its maker can preserve useful cooperation or prevent successors from adapting to changed conditions. The present negotiation concerns how to retain knowledge while releasing an obligation nobody can now perform.

This belongs to The Tour of the Fragrant Ruins. In 1206, a retired basin governor toured southern landing towns to encourage reopening fragrant-bark trade. He held no formal office but his presence changed appointments and credit. A painter accompanying him produced grand images of restored prosperity, while a nearly motionless singer performed laments for workers lost on earlier voyages. Merchants circulated fantastic stories about the bark’s remote source to protect routes and prices. The tour helped revive genuine employment but also bypassed local review of old concessions. Present households compare painted abundance, songs of vulnerability, and actual cargo accounts to decide which promises were made and which audiences merely assumed from a powerful visitor’s approval.

In the connected record, [The Praise Without the Promise](b018-the-praise-without-the-promise.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Signature Before the Praise](b018-the-signature-before-the-praise.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: whether renewed trade can preserve local oversight when the commitments were encouraged by an influential person who signed no accountable undertaking.

Talassar combines salt marshes, sheltered boat towns, and orchards whose irrigation depends on tidal timing. Merchants trade durable claims alongside perishable cargo, although only an actual witnessed undertaking has force after death. A repaired boat, a borrowed name, and a neglected drain can each determine whether a contract remains practical. Houses that prosper from distant obligations are exposed to local maintenance costs they would prefer to call somebody else’s problem. Seasonal kitchens and landing crews keep accounts that often describe the region more accurately than its ceremonial histories of uninterrupted family success.
