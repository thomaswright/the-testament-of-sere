# The Dry Lake Water Portion

*Custom · Namar · Historical coverage: 1081–1248 AFR*

Pilgrims carry water home rather than pour it uselessly onto the dry lake. A small measured portion maintains remembrance. The custom acknowledges scarcity and refuses to make devotion an unlimited demand upon the living wells serving travelers.

The custom is learned through participation rather than a single authoritative text. Households disagree about details while recognizing enough shared actions to cooperate. That flexibility can admit newcomers or allow influential families to redefine the practice in their own favor. Describing it as ancient settles neither possibility. Its practical test is whether a participant can question a demand without losing access to food, water, or witnesses. Local reformers seek finite observances whose costs can be recorded, so that reverence for predecessors does not become an unanswerable demand upon children.

This belongs to The League of the Dry Lake. A caravan kingdom prospered beside a lake whose channels gradually withdrew. After an envoy killed its ruler, patrons renamed the realm and redirected trade. Households dispersed across the basin but retained a league opposing inherited labor constraints. In 1081 a reformer formerly employed by an intrusive court joined a popular uprising and was later executed after promised safe passage failed. Traveling musicians changed styles repeatedly, frustrating demands for one authentic league sound. Rival ensembles attracted patron competition that sometimes overshadowed the shared purpose. The dried lake’s ruins remain a place where migration, solidarity, political compromise, and changing cultural practice are preserved without a single uncontested homeland story.

In the connected record, [The Envoy’s Revised Itinerary](b023-the-envoy-s-revised-itinerary.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Passage Promise Boundary](b023-the-passage-promise-boundary.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: whether the league can protect migrants who lack old lake ancestry without diluting the finite claims its founding households still need recognized.

The Namar passes join pasture settlements to caravan markets. Pack animals carry nearly everything that cannot be made from local stone, wool, or timber. Snow closes crossings irregularly, and a route that is short on a map may cost more food than a longer sheltered path. Hospices preserve undertakings made by travelers who never returned. Their custodians cannot assume that a visitor belongs to one sovereign or one witness tradition. A workable agreement must specify which season, which spring, and which people it protects, rather than claiming a universal welcome the stores cannot support.
