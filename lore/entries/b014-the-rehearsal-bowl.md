# The Rehearsal Bowl

*Artifact · Avel · Historical coverage: 688–1248 AFR*

The bowl collects meal contributions from audiences before featured performances. Its wear shows small donations predominating over famous patrons’ gifts. Keepers display that accounting beside it, making modest sustained support visible within a history otherwise dominated by celebrated discoveries.

Material inspection places limits on the object’s legend. Repairs, wear, and changes in custody can establish that it was used repeatedly without proving every story attached to it. Its value is relational: somebody can authenticate it, somebody needs it for an observance, and somebody may suffer if it is removed. An expensive replacement would not automatically inherit those relations. Custodians therefore record loans and repairs as carefully as ceremonial appearances. A collector who purchases the object but ignores its attached undertaking may own something that cannot safely be taken home.

This belongs to The Harbor of Unlicensed Voices. Dissenters founded a protected harbor in 688 after expulsion from mainland witness cities. They negotiated with existing island households but later charters described the land as providentially empty. A performance keeper welcomed singers excluded by elite patronage and arranged encounters between differing traditions. An esoteric teacher claimed her writings came from a distant master communicating without ordinary speech. Both found sanctuary, though they disagreed about whether unusual inspiration required special authority. Sea toll disputes brought royal intervention. The harbor defended freedom of practice while gradually restricting the island households whose earlier hospitality had made settlement possible. Its present reforms connect freedom of voice to custody of the ground beneath the stage.

In the connected record, [The Skeptic’s Second Verse](b014-the-skeptic-s-second-verse.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Sanctuary Ground Term](b014-the-sanctuary-ground-term.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: whether sanctuary can remain open while restoring the original island households’ role in land and toll decisions.

Avel’s islands preserve competing versions of the basin’s earliest treaties. Sea crossings depend on sails, weather, and provisions, giving a copied text little authority unless somebody can carry its terms home and explain them. Fishing households share beaches with custodians of drowned foundations. Low water reveals masonry that changes both navigation and historical argument. An island’s isolation can shelter dissent and conceal abuse; its effects depend upon who controls the boats. Agreements are usually bounded by named landing places, because a promise to protect an entire sea would have no workable observance.
