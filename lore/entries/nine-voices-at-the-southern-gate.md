# Nine Voices at the Southern Gate

*Current performance and hearing · Vey and Avel · Historical coverage: 1248 AFR*

The Southern Gate amendment hearing begins with a performance in nine voices. Each voice presents one surviving version of Vey’s founding poem, and each actor names the represented reader before speaking. The troupe learned the method from Istren’s borrowed-voice performers, whose uncanny imitations must remain distinguishable from actual testimony. No dead custodian is being summoned. The audience hears how one prestigious text accumulated incompatible conditions through transmission.

Avel’s harbor officials invited the troupe hoping to make charter review seem generous and culturally sophisticated. They expected a smooth conclusion reaffirming the gate’s present authority. The actors instead place the versions beside the harbor’s wordless song and ask which conditions its singers were ever told they endorsed. The Song Is Not Consent Rule supplies a narrow answer: attending a meaningful ceremony did not authenticate unstated charter terms. It does not decide which revised terms should now be accepted.

Care witnesses from the Painted Quarter record who actually supports households harmed by the neighboring spring’s reduced flow. Their evidence includes chosen kin omitted by formal genealogies. This makes compensation more precise and more politically awkward. A household praised as self-supporting turns out to depend upon a person whom its inherited category does not recognize. A royal donor’s apparent generosity turns out to repay part of an older unpaid kitchen obligation.

The Third Bowl’s present keeper has sent a dry account of finite hospitality, not the bowl itself. She will not risk its observance for an elegant traveling symbol. The account helps the gate kitchen specify one night’s meals for claimants without making that welcome a general debt imposed upon every host descendant.

The performance ends without unanimous applause because the hearing has not settled the amendment. Actors leave their role markers on a public table beside the written proposals, making it possible to inspect both interpretation and evidence afterward. In 1248, officials must answer actual claims about representation and spring repair. The nine voices have made uncertainty comprehensible; they have not offered a beautiful replacement for consent. That limited achievement is what the troupe intends to carry home.
