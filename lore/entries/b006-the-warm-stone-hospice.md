# The Warm Stone Hospice

*Place · Namar · Historical coverage: 667–1248 AFR*

The hospice uses sun-warmed masonry and shared bedding to protect travelers without a miraculous heat source. It sheltered soldiers from both succession armies. Royal genealogists omit those mixed occupants, while its supply cords record the food they actually consumed.

The site has changed use more than once. Its surviving structures should not be read as a single intact design: one household repairs what another abandoned, and a later occupant can preserve a wall while giving its opening a different purpose. Visitors who search only for the original foundation overlook the people paying for the present roof. Custody depends on continuing access and maintenance, not merely on a persuasive genealogy. Removing an interesting object may erase evidence of a repair that explains why the place remains habitable at all.

This belongs to The Feast of the Unawakened Heir. For fifty-three years, King Hevren expanded mountain fortresses using labor bound through inherited household pledges. His numerous descendants occupied competing commands. At a private feast in 667, nine senior relatives died and an injured heir was proclaimed sovereign without awakening. A surviving uncle ordered a winter crossing to secure the southern granaries before rival claimants could respond. Pack herds suffered terribly and the army reached its objective unable to carry food home. A satirist later composed three contradictory succession plays. Court readers preserve only the version flattering their patron; caravan households preserve the versions in which servants, animals, and weather determine the outcome.

In the connected record, [The Nine Courtyard Descendants](b006-the-nine-courtyard-descendants.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Sleeping Hand Limit](b006-the-sleeping-hand-limit.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: whether the crown’s descendants can release coerced labor claims without first agreeing which predecessor possessed the authority to make them.

The Namar passes join pasture settlements to caravan markets. Pack animals carry nearly everything that cannot be made from local stone, wool, or timber. Snow closes crossings irregularly, and a route that is short on a map may cost more food than a longer sheltered path. Hospices preserve undertakings made by travelers who never returned. Their custodians cannot assume that a visitor belongs to one sovereign or one witness tradition. A workable agreement must specify which season, which spring, and which people it protects, rather than claiming a universal welcome the stores cannot support.
