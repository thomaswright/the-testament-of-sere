# The Two Forced Abdications

*Event · Namar · Historical coverage: 1072–1248 AFR*

The father yielded to his heir, then both yielded under outside pressure. Officials issued incompatible succession documents within weeks. Ordinary households need clarification of payments made during the interval more urgently than a final elegant account of royal legitimacy.

The event survives through records made for different purposes. A household remembers hunger; a court records jurisdiction; a custodian records the interruption of an observance. Their dates can be reconciled without making their explanations identical. Later commemorations often select a decisive moment because a ceremony needs a beginning and an end. The consequences were less orderly, traveling through marriages, prices, and people who moved away. An honest account follows those consequences far enough to distinguish a temporary outcome from an agreement that still constrains the present.

This belongs to The Raw Music at the Severed Neck. A narrow upland neck connected southern caravan markets to a distant crown that could not reach it easily overland. In 1072 the king entered costly alliances, quarreled with his heir, and underwent successive forced abdications under outside pressure. Local independence leaders secured patron support for control of the neck. A trusted patrol officer then used ordinary access to attack several households before authorities responded, exposing dangerous deference to uniform and office. Raw percussion performers disrupted commemorations that praised order without naming the victims. Their noisy work often offended survivors as well as officials. Present custodians distinguish the freedom to confront public narratives from the practical responsibility to hear people whose loss is being used onstage.

In the connected record, [The Trusted Officer’s Gate Token](b033-the-trusted-officer-s-gate-token.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Uniform Has No Exception](b033-the-uniform-has-no-exception.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: whether the independent neck council can preserve passage and public safety without reproducing the old crown’s unchecked exceptions for trusted officers.

The Namar passes join pasture settlements to caravan markets. Pack animals carry nearly everything that cannot be made from local stone, wool, or timber. Snow closes crossings irregularly, and a route that is short on a map may cost more food than a longer sheltered path. Hospices preserve undertakings made by travelers who never returned. Their custodians cannot assume that a visitor belongs to one sovereign or one witness tradition. A workable agreement must specify which season, which spring, and which people it protects, rather than claiming a universal welcome the stores cannot support.
