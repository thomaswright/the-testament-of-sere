# Oracle Floor Cleaning

*Trade · Namar · Historical coverage: 472–1248 AFR*

Cleaners remove dust, insect nests, and mineral deposits in small sections. The work requires temporary silence and food for displaced performers. Shrine budgets once omitted these costs, treating manual care as devotion rather than a service requiring provisions.

The trade depends upon tools, training, reliable materials, and people who perform work its public account barely mentions. A skilled craftsperson can improve a process, yet every batch still requires hands and a season of available materials. Output remains bounded by fuel, animals, weather, and the hands available to repair equipment. Prices carry the history of these constraints into households far from the workshop. A commercial undertaking that survives its maker can preserve useful cooperation or prevent successors from adapting to changed conditions. The present negotiation concerns how to retain knowledge while releasing an obligation nobody can now perform.

This belongs to The Breath Beneath the Oracle. The oracle at the pass called itself the basin’s center, although its influence depended on pilgrims and rival rulers accepting the claim. In 472 an alchemist heated mineral salts and observed changes in enclosed flame and breath. Shrine workers connected those observations to air moving beneath the oracle’s floor. A family of reciters defended inherited teaching while admitting that ventilation affected performances. An allied army later claimed the oracle predicted victory on the open plateau. The coalition stopped its enemy’s advance but lost a king and exhausted several partners. Neither practical inquiry nor battlefield survival settled which authority could interpret the shrine, leaving a bounded mystery entangled with real work and political need.

In the connected record, [The Victory Oracle Claim](b021-the-victory-oracle-claim.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Inspection Before Interpretation](b021-the-inspection-before-interpretation.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: whether shrine workers can inspect the underfloor channels without being accused of extinguishing the oracle’s voice.

The Namar passes join pasture settlements to caravan markets. Pack animals carry nearly everything that cannot be made from local stone, wool, or timber. Snow closes crossings irregularly, and a route that is short on a map may cost more food than a longer sheltered path. Hospices preserve undertakings made by travelers who never returned. Their custodians cannot assume that a visitor belongs to one sovereign or one witness tradition. A workable agreement must specify which season, which spring, and which people it protects, rather than claiming a universal welcome the stores cannot support.
