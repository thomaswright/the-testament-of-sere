# The Judge’s Empty Footsteps

*Phenomenon · Suth · Historical coverage: 943–1248 AFR*

Footsteps recur on an abandoned hearing path after a claimant dies unanswered. Rain channels explain some sounds but not their timing. Judges have begun recording postponed cases publicly, responding to an actual unfinished duty without claiming certainty about the phenomenon.

Witnesses distinguish an observed effect from their explanation for it. Repetition under comparable conditions is valuable evidence; a striking story told after its alleged cause becomes famous is weaker evidence. Masons, gardeners, and boat crews often recognize patterns an interpreter overlooks. Their explanation may reduce the immediate danger without settling whether an undertaking contributed to it. The basin has no institution capable of issuing a final answer for every manifestation. Communities record the conditions under which the effect occurs, then negotiate a response whose costs they can actually sustain.

This belongs to The Judges Who Walk Between Gardens. Suth’s garden communes elected judges rather than inheriting a sovereign. In 943 a physician reformer sought to reduce the separate courts maintained by armed households and funerary custodians. His ally withdrew support when those institutions threatened revolt. Traveling musicians carried arguments between valleys, blending older measures with instruments learned in exile. An outside authority later occupied the eastern gardens while the western communes retained a different charter. When a public vote ended occupation, departing forces destroyed channels and records. Returning households therefore faced reconstruction before constitutional celebration. The elected judges now walk between settlements to hear claims, because neither the old exemptions nor the new vote can make water flow through a broken canal.

In the connected record, [Graft Recovery Teaching](b020-graft-recovery-teaching.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Coerced Transfer Review](b020-the-coerced-transfer-review.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: how elected courts can rebuild channels while deciding which occupation-era transactions were coerced and which present households depend upon them.

The Suth valleys cultivate grafted orchards and irrigated gardens amid dry hills. Food production depends on precise local knowledge: when to open a channel, which rootstock tolerates mineral water, and how many hands a harvest requires. Asylum customs let strangers work under temporary names, but temporary recognition does not automatically convey inheritance. The same gardens can preserve an old promise and expose its injustice. A court that assigns water without accounting for the labor of keeping it clean may issue a valid judgment that no household can afford to obey.
