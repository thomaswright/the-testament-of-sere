# The Register of Absences

*Archive · Vey · Historical coverage: 1216–1248 AFR*

The Register of Absences is a collection of questions arranged as if they were household records. Each leaf names a place, a date, an unexplained transaction, and the evidence still needed to interpret it. Vesh invented the format after finding that ordinary archives encouraged researchers to fill a blank with the most satisfying available story. His register instead gives the blank a permanent address. A reader can add evidence without pretending that the question has already been answered.

The first leaf concerns four extra bowls purchased for a house that officially held two people. Subsequent leaves cover ferry fares paid after a recorded death, a child apprenticed under two incompatible names, and rent supplied by the wrong military treasury. Some mysteries have harmless solutions. A widow kept her dead husband's trade name because changing it would invalidate her stall lease. A ferryman's date marks followed a local flood calendar rather than the common Reckoning. Solving these cases prevents them from becoming evidence for an imagined conspiracy.

Other leaves document genuine coercion. Several tenants in the hidden chambers disappeared just before their assumed names were transferred to paying clients. Vesh records the probable pattern but marks the individual fates as unknown. Families sometimes find this refusal unbearable. He offers them separate memorial leaves on which a hope or recollection can be preserved without being confused with established fact. The register thus contains grief beside evidence, distinguished by the color of the cord binding each section.

Courts have begun accepting references to the register, which presents a new problem: a wealthy claimant can commission repeated investigations into a poor neighbor's identity. Vesh now requires a public reason for any inquiry seeking to remove an existing right. That rule slows useful work as well as abusive work. In 1248 the collection occupies two cupboards and needs a trained successor. Its achievement is not the number of names recovered but the number of convenient conclusions it has prevented from becoming somebody else's inherited misfortune.
