# The Reference Spoken Twice

*Custom · Vey · Historical coverage: 1176–1248 AFR*

Readers state both a page’s claim and the location of supporting evidence. The custom slows recitation but exposes unsupported authority. Sensitive locations are supplied through a trusted witness rather than shouted publicly, preserving a finite separation between accountability and exposure.

The custom is learned through participation rather than a single authoritative text. Households disagree about details while recognizing enough shared actions to cooperate. That flexibility can admit newcomers or allow influential families to redefine the practice in their own favor. Describing it as ancient settles neither possibility. Its practical test is whether a participant can question a demand without losing access to food, water, or witnesses. Local reformers seek finite observances whose costs can be recorded, so that reverence for predecessors does not become an unanswerable demand upon children.

This belongs to The Theater of Linked Doors. After a long-serving magistrate withdrew in 1176, rival councils shared offices without agreeing who could issue orders. A traveling dramatist used colored lanterns and hinged scenery to stage judgments between living witnesses and dead claimants. Another performer, trained abroad, brought stories about inherited knowledge denied to younger relatives. Readers connected their manuscript copies through explicit page references, allowing an account in one town to point to evidence held in another. Patrols seized central catalogs but could not erase copies already dispersed. The network preserved testimony and also exposed vulnerable households when careless references revealed their locations. Its keepers now negotiate the difference between public evidence and information that enables coercion.

In the connected record, [The Catalog with Broken Returns](b015-the-catalog-with-broken-returns.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Withheld Location Clause](b015-the-withheld-location-clause.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: which references should remain public when making testimony accessible can also identify people a patrol wants to seize.

Vey households measure wealth partly in dependable access to a public landing. Their markets exchange grain, lamp oil, glazed jars, and legal testimony. A witness can authenticate a transaction but cannot provide the labor needed to fulfill it. Seasonal crews clean the same embankments on which courts announce judgments, and a decision that ignores those crews soon meets an expensive physical limit. Flood deposits preserve small objects while destroying the arrangement that once made their meaning obvious. Recovering an object is therefore easier than establishing what anybody was entitled to do with it.
