# The Refuge Meal Named

*Custom · Vey · Historical coverage: 591–1248 AFR*

Hosts name both refugee contributions and local assistance during commemorative meals. The practice avoids portraying either group as passive. Disputes remain over unpaid loans, making acknowledgment a beginning of accounting rather than a substitute for it.

The custom is learned through participation rather than a single authoritative text. Households disagree about details while recognizing enough shared actions to cooperate. That flexibility can admit newcomers or allow influential families to redefine the practice in their own favor. Describing it as ancient settles neither possibility. Its practical test is whether a participant can question a demand without losing access to food, water, or witnesses. Local reformers seek finite observances whose costs can be recorded, so that reverence for predecessors does not become an unanswerable demand upon children.

This belongs to The Capital Carried Downriver. Highland households fleeing famine joined beneath a negotiator who secured refuge through protection agreements rather than claiming an empty landscape. In 591 a dynasty moved its court downriver and founded a new capital beside the main grain landing. Its ceremonies announced victory before walls and drains were complete. A performer portraying a workshop organizer helped laborers compare the unequal terms under which they built the city. An appointed governor then joined distant dependencies into one jurisdiction to redirect unofficial trade. The new center gained real commercial strength while straining older refuge agreements. Present descendants occupy a layered street where royal splendor, migrant survival, and ordinary collective bargaining cannot be cleanly separated.

In the connected record, [The Street in Four Courts](b022-the-street-in-four-courts.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Protection Is Not Succession Clause](b022-the-protection-is-not-succession-clause.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: whether capital households can revise assessments without extinguishing the finite protection agreements under which highland refugees first settled.

Vey households measure wealth partly in dependable access to a public landing. Their markets exchange grain, lamp oil, glazed jars, and legal testimony. A witness can authenticate a transaction but cannot provide the labor needed to fulfill it. Seasonal crews clean the same embankments on which courts announce judgments, and a decision that ignores those crews soon meets an expensive physical limit. Flood deposits preserve small objects while destroying the arrangement that once made their meaning obvious. Recovering an object is therefore easier than establishing what anybody was entitled to do with it.
