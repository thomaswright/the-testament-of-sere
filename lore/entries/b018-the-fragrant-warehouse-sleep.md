# The Fragrant Warehouse Sleep

*Phenomenon · Talassar · Historical coverage: 1206–1248 AFR*

Workers report shared dreams of ships waiting without crews after reopening an old store. Ventilation reduced some symptoms, while dreams persisted among custodians of unfulfilled cargo pledges. Investigators separate exposure from testimony rather than treating either explanation as sufficient for all cases.

Witnesses distinguish an observed effect from their explanation for it. Repetition under comparable conditions is valuable evidence; a striking story told after its alleged cause becomes famous is weaker evidence. Masons, gardeners, and boat crews often recognize patterns an interpreter overlooks. Their explanation may reduce the immediate danger without settling whether an undertaking contributed to it. The basin has no institution capable of issuing a final answer for every manifestation. Communities record the conditions under which the effect occurs, then negotiate a response whose costs they can actually sustain.

This belongs to The Tour of the Fragrant Ruins. In 1206, a retired basin governor toured southern landing towns to encourage reopening fragrant-bark trade. He held no formal office but his presence changed appointments and credit. A painter accompanying him produced grand images of restored prosperity, while a nearly motionless singer performed laments for workers lost on earlier voyages. Merchants circulated fantastic stories about the bark’s remote source to protect routes and prices. The tour helped revive genuine employment but also bypassed local review of old concessions. Present households compare painted abundance, songs of vulnerability, and actual cargo accounts to decide which promises were made and which audiences merely assumed from a powerful visitor’s approval.

In the connected record, [Bark Resting and Stripping](b018-bark-resting-and-stripping.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Signature Before the Praise](b018-the-signature-before-the-praise.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: whether renewed trade can preserve local oversight when the commitments were encouraged by an influential person who signed no accountable undertaking.

Talassar combines salt marshes, sheltered boat towns, and orchards whose irrigation depends on tidal timing. Merchants trade durable claims alongside perishable cargo, although only an actual witnessed undertaking has force after death. A repaired boat, a borrowed name, and a neglected drain can each determine whether a contract remains practical. Houses that prosper from distant obligations are exposed to local maintenance costs they would prefer to call somebody else’s problem. Seasonal kitchens and landing crews keep accounts that often describe the region more accurately than its ceremonial histories of uninterrupted family success.
