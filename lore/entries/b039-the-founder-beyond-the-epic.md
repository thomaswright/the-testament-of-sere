# The Founder Beyond the Epic

*Text · Suth · Historical coverage: 779–1248 AFR*

The booklet compares oral episodes with independent travel and tribute records. Some details align and others remain unconfirmed. Readers preserve that pattern, allowing historical existence to be established without requiring every dramatic event to become literal fact.

Copies disagree in ways that matter. A shortened phrase can remove a condition, and a copied date can imply that a later amendment belonged to the original undertaking. Dry writing preserves evidence but cannot make an oath binding by itself. Readers must distinguish a record of consent from a writer’s assertion that consent occurred. Marginal notes are sometimes the only surviving testimony of people excluded from the formal version. Establishing a trustworthy reading therefore depends on comparing custody, material, and ordinary expenses, as well as elegant arguments about wording.

This belongs to The Epic and the Unoccupied Garden. An upland founder’s life survived through performed epic alongside sparse independent records. Later households used the epic to justify incompatible claims to a fertile valley. In 779 a negotiator established a garden zone from which armed officers withdrew for talks, hoping protected meeting space would end recurring conflict. Outside coalitions continued contesting its borders and access. A naval battle far downstream redirected trade, weakening sponsors who had supplied the negotiations and strengthening new patrons. The peace zone became both a genuine refuge and a place where rivals accumulated influence. Present gardeners compare heroic memory, signed terms, and changed supply routes to preserve talks without pretending withdrawal of one force made the valley free of coercion.

In the connected record, [The Garden Border Hoopoe](b039-the-garden-border-hoopoe.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Resident Within Neutrality](b039-the-resident-within-neutrality.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: whether the meeting garden can remain protected when the trade revenues supporting its food and repairs now pass through rival downstream hands.

The Suth valleys cultivate grafted orchards and irrigated gardens amid dry hills. Food production depends on precise local knowledge: when to open a channel, which rootstock tolerates mineral water, and how many hands a harvest requires. Asylum customs let strangers work under temporary names, but temporary recognition does not automatically convey inheritance. The same gardens can preserve an old promise and expose its injustice. A court that assigns water without accounting for the labor of keeping it clean may issue a valid judgment that no household can afford to obey.
