# The Shared Script Hall

*Place · Avel · Historical coverage: 914–1248 AFR*

The hall taught a patron’s formal language while everyday accounts remained in several local tongues. Students learned to translate obligations rather than erase differences. Later custodians banned vernacular margins, weakening the very accuracy that justified the school’s prestige.

The site has changed use more than once. Its surviving structures should not be read as a single intact design: one household repairs what another abandoned, and a later occupant can preserve a wall while giving its opening a different purpose. Visitors who search only for the original foundation overlook the people paying for the present roof. Custody depends on continuing access and maintenance, not merely on a persuasive genealogy. Removing an interesting object may erase evidence of a repair that explains why the place remains habitable at all.

This belongs to The Houses of Unequal Stars. In 914, island reformers built a community where work rotated according to declared inclination rather than inherited household status. An observer arranged stars by color and apparent brightness, discovering that similar lights could stand at very different distances. Enthusiasts assigned household tasks according to the star table, converting a warning against superficial resemblance into a system of fixed personal destiny. Mainland patrons supplied scribes and introduced a prestigious administrative language distinct from everyday speech. A peace conference divided the islands temporarily between rival custodians. The promised review never occurred, and the community’s experiments became competing traditions on opposite sides of a supposedly provisional line.

In the connected record, [The Unranked Observers](b007-the-unranked-observers.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Seasonal Partition Term](b007-the-seasonal-partition-term.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: whether a common work calendar can cross the old partition without restoring either the star castes or the patrons’ exclusive scribal authority.

Avel’s islands preserve competing versions of the basin’s earliest treaties. Sea crossings depend on sails, weather, and provisions, giving a copied text little authority unless somebody can carry its terms home and explain them. Fishing households share beaches with custodians of drowned foundations. Low water reveals masonry that changes both navigation and historical argument. An island’s isolation can shelter dissent and conceal abuse; its effects depend upon who controls the boats. Agreements are usually bounded by named landing places, because a promise to protect an entire sea would have no workable observance.
