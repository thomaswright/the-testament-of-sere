# The Stone Compared Before Praise

*Custom · Namar · Historical coverage: 1034–1248 AFR*

Readers compare hardness, cleavage, and weathering before telling a sample’s celebrated history. The practice allows ordinary material to receive accurate attention. It also prevents a rare origin story from overriding evidence that a stone is unsafe for a particular construction task.

The custom is learned through participation rather than a single authoritative text. Households disagree about details while recognizing enough shared actions to cooperate. That flexibility can admit newcomers or allow influential families to redefine the practice in their own favor. Describing it as ancient settles neither possibility. Its practical test is whether a participant can question a demand without losing access to food, water, or witnesses. Local reformers seek finite observances whose costs can be recorded, so that reverence for predecessors does not become an unanswerable demand upon children.

This belongs to The Milk Names and the Stone Families. Pasture households recognized milk kin through feeding and care as well as descent. A visiting reader tried to arrange those relations into universal stages, preserving useful terms while making unwarranted claims about every society’s development. In 1034 a modest ruler supported milk cooperatives and dropped a prestigious inherited name, hoping practical provision would cross old ranks. Mineral workers compared stones through visible properties instead of legendary virtue. A coastal campaign then constricted the few harbors supplying the passes, turning ordinary transport difficulty into severe shortage. The cooperative survived through finite sharing and adapted kin obligations. Present readers compare social categories and mineral forms without assuming either classification supplies a single ladder of worth.

In the connected record, [The Harbor Shortages Inland](b043-the-harbor-shortages-inland.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Feeding Is Not Ownership Clause](b043-the-feeding-is-not-ownership-clause.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: whether milk kin obligations can include newcomers during shortage without becoming permanent claims upon children whose feeding was initially emergency assistance.

The Namar passes join pasture settlements to caravan markets. Pack animals carry nearly everything that cannot be made from local stone, wool, or timber. Snow closes crossings irregularly, and a route that is short on a map may cost more food than a longer sheltered path. Hospices preserve undertakings made by travelers who never returned. Their custodians cannot assume that a visitor belongs to one sovereign or one witness tradition. A workable agreement must specify which season, which spring, and which people it protects, rather than claiming a universal welcome the stores cannot support.
