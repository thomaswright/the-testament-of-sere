# The Side Channel Compact

*Law · Orun · Historical coverage: 859–1248 AFR*

The Side Channel Compact ended seventy-seven years of argument about who should repair the flood damage caused by the Heret ambush. Its signatories included reed cutters, ferry households, burial custodians, and the Green Tithe's counting houses. The military coalitions were excluded because neither would acknowledge that the Unannounced War had legally occurred. Their refusal, intended to avoid compensation, deprived them of any voice in the channel's subsequent governance.

The compact assigns maintenance by benefit rather than ownership. A field owner contributes according to the land protected from flooding. A ferryman contributes according to the number of crossings made. Burial custodians contribute labor only when a repair threatens grave ground. Counting houses contribute a portion of stored reed because the channel allows their warehouses to remain usable. These different measures require frequent conversion, and the conversion table is the compact's most fought-over annex.

Its unusual clause allows a contributor to dispute an assessment while continuing essential repairs. Earlier agreements treated refusal to pay as refusal to honor the whole undertaking, making a minor accounting disagreement capable of provoking a supernatural crisis. The compact separates the unquestioned task from the contested expense. Courts elsewhere cite this distinction, although wealthy parties often resist it when it would prevent them from stopping work to strengthen a bargaining position.

In 1248, the channel's upper gate is failing again. The original maintenance schedule assumed a rainfall pattern that no longer holds, and the burial terrace has expanded into the only safe approach for heavy timber. The compact can be amended, but each party demands evidence before accepting a new measure of benefit. Meanwhile, cutters reinforce the gate with basketwork and ferry households transport stone without charging the full rate. They record these contributions in a temporary account. Their immediate cooperation preserves the possibility of a later dispute, which is exactly the kind of imperfect success the compact was designed to permit.
