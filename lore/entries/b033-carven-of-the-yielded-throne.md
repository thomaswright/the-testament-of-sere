# Carven of the Yielded Throne

*Person · Namar · Historical coverage: 1072–1248 AFR*

Carven sought money through alliances that narrowed his choices. His conflict with his heir became public theater. Personal accounts show dependence on advisers and creditors, making political responsibility larger than the convenient image of one uniquely foolish monarch.

Later accounts make Carven of the Yielded Throne the explanation for an entire local change. The surviving record instead places this life among dependencies: an introduction supplied by another household, materials purchased on credit, and testimony given by people with reasons of their own. A biography that removes those dependencies becomes a useful legend and a poor guide to what happened. Descendants inherit evidence and expectations, rather than the ability to repeat an ancestor’s achievement. The practical question is which part of the achievement can be maintained by ordinary people after the celebrated individual is gone.

This belongs to The Raw Music at the Severed Neck. A narrow upland neck connected southern caravan markets to a distant crown that could not reach it easily overland. In 1072 the king entered costly alliances, quarreled with his heir, and underwent successive forced abdications under outside pressure. Local independence leaders secured patron support for control of the neck. A trusted patrol officer then used ordinary access to attack several households before authorities responded, exposing dangerous deference to uniform and office. Raw percussion performers disrupted commemorations that praised order without naming the victims. Their noisy work often offended survivors as well as officials. Present custodians distinguish the freedom to confront public narratives from the practical responsibility to hear people whose loss is being used onstage.

In the connected record, [The Victims’ Late Memorial Yard](b033-the-victims-late-memorial-yard.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Uniform Has No Exception](b033-the-uniform-has-no-exception.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: whether the independent neck council can preserve passage and public safety without reproducing the old crown’s unchecked exceptions for trusted officers.

The Namar passes join pasture settlements to caravan markets. Pack animals carry nearly everything that cannot be made from local stone, wool, or timber. Snow closes crossings irregularly, and a route that is short on a map may cost more food than a longer sheltered path. Hospices preserve undertakings made by travelers who never returned. Their custodians cannot assume that a visitor belongs to one sovereign or one witness tradition. A workable agreement must specify which season, which spring, and which people it protects, rather than claiming a universal welcome the stores cannot support.
