# The Cargo That Arrived as a Forest

*Disaster and route · Talassar · Historical coverage: 1206–1248 AFR*

The cargo arrived as bundled bark, wrapped in coarse cloth and loaded beneath sacks of millet. It never became a living forest. The familiar name comes from the warehouse keepers' account: while the bark remained inside, footsteps sounded among trunks, ropes rasped against unseen branches, and a person searching for the far wall sometimes returned with scratches no surviving beam could explain. The warehouse measured seventeen ordinary paces across. None of the scratches contained a thorn.

The bark had been stripped at a landing whose sellers insisted upon naming the growing households in every subsequent sale. A Talassari intermediary erased those names to protect the source from competitors. He retained their witnessed promise to supply the next harvest while representing the cargo as his own unrestricted possession. The expedition's water jar, used to authenticate delivery, traveled with the bundles. Whether that combination caused the disturbance remains disputed; inspection established it before anybody proposed an explanation.

Opening the doors and removing six bundles quieted one corner. Burning a bundle brought ordinary smoke and worsened the voices heard near the floor. The keepers then separated the cargo into dry sheds and sent the jar back with an account of its custody. They paid for three named witnesses to travel from the producers' landing. During the resulting inventory, the sounds dwindled until only one faint rope scrape remained beside a repaired drain. The drain lost water for the rest of the season. The missing volume could be measured. A punishment could not.

Grosen painted the governor standing before an open warehouse, with invented healthy trees behind him. The picture implied that the visiting ruler had made abundance safe through courage. It omitted the keepers' soaked bedding, the failed burn, and the suppliers who returned to correct the account. Orven's lament was commissioned for the same welcome feast. He sang without moving, naming the distance a household must travel to recover a word erased by someone richer. Several patrons left before the final verse.

In 1248 the warehouse contains net floats and spare planking. Boat crews knock on its threshold before entering, a habit with no verified supernatural effect. The old cloth wraps survive in the cargo readers' room, each attached to a plain statement of where it was used. Traders still sell extravagant accounts of a bark forest hidden beyond the sea. Pilots prefer the recorded route, whose hazards include bad weather and unpaid suppliers. The surviving scrape is heard chiefly after heavy rain. A mason believes a loose fitting explains it; the keeper leaves both accounts open and repairs the drain.
