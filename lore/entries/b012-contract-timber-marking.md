# Contract Timber Marking

*Trade · Talassar · Historical coverage: 1123–1248 AFR*

Suppliers mark beams before delivery and retain matching cut-offs. Those ordinary practices enabled the house investigation. New officials seek standardized royal stamps, while craftsmen argue that independent marks provide evidence precisely because no one authority controls every copy.

The trade depends upon tools, training, reliable materials, and people who perform work its public account barely mentions. A skilled craftsperson can improve a process, yet every batch still requires hands and a season of available materials. Output remains bounded by fuel, animals, weather, and the hands available to repair equipment. Prices carry the history of these constraints into households far from the workshop. A commercial undertaking that survives its maker can preserve useful cooperation or prevent successors from adapting to changed conditions. The present negotiation concerns how to retain knowledge while releasing an obligation nobody can now perform.

This belongs to The Empty Cloth Negotiations. After several seasons of falling prices in 1123, Talassar merchants reduced wages while their stored obligations retained old values. A mural painter simplified laboring figures into bright curved forms visible across crowded quays. A public reader investigated a councilor’s splendid house and found that its builder also received favorable canal contracts. The reader lost her sponsored platform but continued recitations in market yards. Negotiators began carrying plain cloth to request safe passage, although some courts treated the sign as surrender and others as a request to speak. Confusion allowed creditors to seize people approaching in good faith. Shared standards emerged slowly from recorded incidents rather than a single heroic proclamation.

In the connected record, [The Old Debt Price Review](b012-the-old-debt-price-review.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Approach Without Submission](b012-the-approach-without-submission.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: how negotiated access and old debts can be revised while households still need the contracts maintaining their canals.

Talassar combines salt marshes, sheltered boat towns, and orchards whose irrigation depends on tidal timing. Merchants trade durable claims alongside perishable cargo, although only an actual witnessed undertaking has force after death. A repaired boat, a borrowed name, and a neglected drain can each determine whether a contract remains practical. Houses that prosper from distant obligations are exposed to local maintenance costs they would prefer to call somebody else’s problem. Seasonal kitchens and landing crews keep accounts that often describe the region more accurately than its ceremonial histories of uninterrupted family success.
