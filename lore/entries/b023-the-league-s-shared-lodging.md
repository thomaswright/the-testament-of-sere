# The League’s Shared Lodging

*Place · Namar · Historical coverage: 1081–1248 AFR*

Migrant families share a lodge funded through small subscriptions. Ancestry advocates want priority beds. Keepers separate emergency shelter from old claim research, allowing help without turning every arrival into a false assertion of inherited lake rights.

The site has changed use more than once. Its surviving structures should not be read as a single intact design: one household repairs what another abandoned, and a later occupant can preserve a wall while giving its opening a different purpose. Visitors who search only for the original foundation overlook the people paying for the present roof. Custody depends on continuing access and maintenance, not merely on a persuasive genealogy. Removing an interesting object may erase evidence of a repair that explains why the place remains habitable at all.

This belongs to The League of the Dry Lake. A caravan kingdom prospered beside a lake whose channels gradually withdrew. After an envoy killed its ruler, patrons renamed the realm and redirected trade. Households dispersed across the basin but retained a league opposing inherited labor constraints. In 1081 a reformer formerly employed by an intrusive court joined a popular uprising and was later executed after promised safe passage failed. Traveling musicians changed styles repeatedly, frustrating demands for one authentic league sound. Rival ensembles attracted patron competition that sometimes overshadowed the shared purpose. The dried lake’s ruins remain a place where migration, solidarity, political compromise, and changing cultural practice are preserved without a single uncontested homeland story.

In the connected record, [The Rival Lake Ensembles](b023-the-rival-lake-ensembles.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Passage Promise Boundary](b023-the-passage-promise-boundary.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: whether the league can protect migrants who lack old lake ancestry without diluting the finite claims its founding households still need recognized.

The Namar passes join pasture settlements to caravan markets. Pack animals carry nearly everything that cannot be made from local stone, wool, or timber. Snow closes crossings irregularly, and a route that is short on a map may cost more food than a longer sheltered path. Hospices preserve undertakings made by travelers who never returned. Their custodians cannot assume that a visitor belongs to one sovereign or one witness tradition. A workable agreement must specify which season, which spring, and which people it protects, rather than claiming a universal welcome the stores cannot support.
