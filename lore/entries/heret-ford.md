# Heret Ford

*Place · Orun · Historical coverage: 612–1248 AFR*

Heret Ford is only a ford during the six driest weeks of the year. At other times, boats cross above a submerged line of limestone slabs whose original purpose is uncertain. The slabs divide the current into shallow tongues, making the location useful for unloading heavy cargo without a proper quay. Their worn marks resemble writing but have never been read consistently. The Green Tithe's collectors insist they are an ancient grant. Pilots note that four were turned upside down during repairs.

In 782, reed cutters opened a forgotten channel above the crossing and sent a wall of water into three armed inspection barges. The military account describes an ambush by hidden warriors. Canal maintenance rolls show that the same families had petitioned for years to repair the channel's failing gate. They used a dangerous structure they understood because the authorities had refused to maintain it. Their knowledge became celebrated only after it became useful in war.

The victory damaged the ford more seriously than the invaders did. Floodwater tore away the southern bank and exposed old graves, releasing competing observances into the crossing's already crowded water. For seventy years, ferry operators had to pour a measure upstream and another into a separate basin on shore. Combining them produced a pressure that cracked the boat's caulking. Whether this was a supernatural incompatibility or a particularly corrosive mineral deposit remains a subject of practical argument. The operators continue separating the water because it works.

Heret's present market sells replacement ropes, pottery strainers, and seasonal licenses. A child can earn a little money marking safe shallows, but the town's elders forbid selling routes to strangers without an adult pilot. The prohibition followed three avoidable drownings, not an ancient curse. Tourists come to see the battlefield slabs and often leave disappointed by their ordinary appearance. The inhabitants regard that disappointment as welcome: the ford needs people willing to pay for transport, not visitors expecting history to announce itself in the shape of a sword.
