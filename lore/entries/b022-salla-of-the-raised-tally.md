# Salla of the Raised Tally

*Person · Vey · Historical coverage: 591–1248 AFR*

Salla portrayed a worker holding an expense tally instead of a sword. Audiences copied the gesture at wage meetings. Patrons praised its dignity while requesting that performances avoid the contracts currently burdening their own workshops.

Later accounts make Salla of the Raised Tally the explanation for an entire local change. The surviving record instead places this life among dependencies: an introduction supplied by another household, materials purchased on credit, and testimony given by people with reasons of their own. A biography that removes those dependencies becomes a useful legend and a poor guide to what happened. Descendants inherit evidence and expectations, rather than the ability to repeat an ancestor’s achievement. The practical question is which part of the achievement can be maintained by ordinary people after the celebrated individual is gone.

This belongs to The Capital Carried Downriver. Highland households fleeing famine joined beneath a negotiator who secured refuge through protection agreements rather than claiming an empty landscape. In 591 a dynasty moved its court downriver and founded a new capital beside the main grain landing. Its ceremonies announced victory before walls and drains were complete. A performer portraying a workshop organizer helped laborers compare the unequal terms under which they built the city. An appointed governor then joined distant dependencies into one jurisdiction to redirect unofficial trade. The new center gained real commercial strength while straining older refuge agreements. Present descendants occupy a layered street where royal splendor, migrant survival, and ordinary collective bargaining cannot be cleanly separated.

In the connected record, [The Victorious Unfinished Street](b022-the-victorious-unfinished-street.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Protection Is Not Succession Clause](b022-the-protection-is-not-succession-clause.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: whether capital households can revise assessments without extinguishing the finite protection agreements under which highland refugees first settled.

Vey households measure wealth partly in dependable access to a public landing. Their markets exchange grain, lamp oil, glazed jars, and legal testimony. A witness can authenticate a transaction but cannot provide the labor needed to fulfill it. Seasonal crews clean the same embankments on which courts announce judgments, and a decision that ignores those crews soon meets an expensive physical limit. Flood deposits preserve small objects while destroying the arrangement that once made their meaning obvious. Recovering an object is therefore easier than establishing what anybody was entitled to do with it.
