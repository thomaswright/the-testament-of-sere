# The Unannounced War

*Event · Orun · Historical coverage: 781–789 AFR*

The Unannounced War began when the lower river's toll collectors stopped accepting witness tokens issued upstream. No ruler proclaimed hostilities. Each city called its armed boats inspectors, and each denied responsibility for the burned granaries discovered after inspections. The distinction mattered to the dead: military requisitions could suspend certain private undertakings, whereas an ordinary inspection could not. Soldiers therefore carried legal opinions into combat, hoping a favorable interpretation would make an enemy's ancestor withdraw protection from a landing.

The first serious fighting occurred at Heret Ford in 782. A company of reed cutters, whose families had been pledged to the Green Tithe, sank three inspection barges by opening a neglected side channel. Their victory convinced upland towns that the monopoly could be broken cheaply. Seven harvests later, those towns had paid for an army they could no longer feed. The lower coalition had lost almost every engagement on dry ground but retained the grain boats, the ferry witnesses, and the ability to close the estuary in winter.

The settlement of 789 awarded the upper towns the ceremonial right to wash their seals in the estuary. It left the monopoly's pricing and labor claims intact. The returning commanders displayed silver bowls as evidence of victory; hungry households displayed empty bowls outside the councils. Within a year, four councils had fallen and two victorious captains had been expelled from the cities they claimed to have liberated.

Present historians disagree about which battle ended the war because they first disagree about whether it was legally a war. Its clearest surviving legacy is mundane: river pilots still keep two cargo lists, one showing what a boat carries and another showing which authority has acknowledged the people aboard. A discrepancy can delay a vessel for days. In 1248, an unacknowledged passenger is more likely to drown waiting for a ruling than to be struck by an ancestral curse. That ordinary risk is the campaign's most durable monument.
