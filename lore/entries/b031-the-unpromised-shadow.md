# The Unpromised Shadow

*Phenomenon · Suth · Historical coverage: 631–1248 AFR*

A parlor visitor’s shadow remains briefly beside a memorial basin after she leaves. Lantern tests reproduce most reports, but one occurs in daylight. Keepers refuse to identify it as a tale creature, recording a finite unresolved effect instead of letting fiction dictate evidence.

Witnesses distinguish an observed effect from their explanation for it. Repetition under comparable conditions is valuable evidence; a striking story told after its alleged cause becomes famous is weaker evidence. Masons, gardeners, and boat crews often recognize patterns an interpreter overlooks. Their explanation may reduce the immediate danger without settling whether an undertaking contributed to it. The basin has no institution capable of issuing a final answer for every manifestation. Communities record the conditions under which the effect occurs, then negotiate a response whose costs they can actually sustain.

This belongs to The Women Who Named the Hidden Country. Garden migrants carried a coastal place name inland until it became the name of several distinct settlements. Later patrons suppressed their administrative institutions but could not erase household speech. In 631 a materials negotiator organized scarce timber and metal during war, then sought agreements with former enemies. He was murdered amid accusations about his outsider ancestry. Women gathering in garden parlors wrote elaborate wonder tales whose hidden countries unsettled the courts’ official map of belonging. A language revival collected their work beside older oral accounts, sometimes mistaking polished literary invention for unchanged folklore. Present readers preserve both forms and question which histories were chosen to make a people appear uniform after migration.

In the connected record, [Revival Dictionary Copying](b031-revival-dictionary-copying.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Speech Without Descent Test](b031-the-speech-without-descent-test.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: whether revived language institutions can welcome their actual mixed descendants without replacing old exclusion with a newly polished ancestry test.

The Suth valleys cultivate grafted orchards and irrigated gardens amid dry hills. Food production depends on precise local knowledge: when to open a channel, which rootstock tolerates mineral water, and how many hands a harvest requires. Asylum customs let strangers work under temporary names, but temporary recognition does not automatically convey inheritance. The same gardens can preserve an old promise and expose its injustice. A court that assigns water without accounting for the labor of keeping it clean may issue a valid judgment that no household can afford to obey.
