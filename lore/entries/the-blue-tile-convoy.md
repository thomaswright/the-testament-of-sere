# The Blue Tile Convoy

*Current journey · Orun, Namar, and Istren · Historical coverage: 1247–1248 AFR*

The Blue Tile Convoy carries forty-six washing markers from Orun’s wards to Istren’s northern depots. It is less impressive than its painted banner suggests. Half the tiles traveled by shallow boat, several cracked during unloading, and the remaining crates now rest beneath a leaking hospice roof in Namar. Its leader, the tile keeper Seta, has counted every usable piece rather than preserve the original figure as a promise already fulfilled.

Northern depot households requested the tiles after travelers repeatedly confused clean water jars with salted washing stores. Orun’s keepers agreed to a finite shipment in exchange for dried fish and replacement cloth. Sweet Town workers added seed paste intended for the journey, not as a substitute for staple food. At the pass, a milk cooperative fed stranded carriers during snow closure and asked that the assistance become a standing claim upon their apprentices. Seta accepted the meal and refused the expansion. The Feeding Is Not Ownership Clause gives her language for the refusal, but no food with which to make it easy.

The convoy now depends on a narrower agreement. Carriers will repair the cooperative’s drying frames for three days after the road opens. The cooperative will provide a specified amount of fodder and milk for that interval. Both keep dry copies, and actual witnesses will authenticate the exchange at a spring unrelated to older contested claims. This arrangement is slow because it records what each group can provide instead of invoking one beneficent authority’s general generosity.

An Istren depot patron has offered faster passage in exchange for putting his emblem on every tile. Seta objects that the marking would imply he supplied work already performed in Orun. The depot’s own keepers propose a separate contribution line naming his actual transport assistance. He has not answered.

In 1248, the crates remain in Namar, their blue faces appearing bright only when ordinary daylight reaches them. A cracked tile has been set beside the hospice washing basin as a local replacement, with consent from the northern recipients. The reduced shipment and increased useful provision will both appear in the final account. The convoy’s success will consist of clean stations actually maintained, not an unchanged number carried into a triumphant story about effortless aid.
