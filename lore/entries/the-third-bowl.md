# The Third Bowl

*Ritual object · Vey · Historical coverage: 776–1248 AFR*

The Third Bowl is an ordinary earthen vessel with a thumb-sized repair in its rim. During the reconciliation meetings of 776, delegates placed two bowls between them: one containing water from the upstream springs, the other water from the estuary. Witness Maren added a third filled from the public kitchen. Her argument was that agreements made between authorities should also be witnessed by the water used to feed people who held no authority. The kitchen servants approved; the delegates initially treated the addition as an eccentric courtesy.

Maren's murder three meetings later made the object famous. A knife entered beneath her ribs as she leaned forward to pour. Her blood reached the kitchen bowl, while the vessels belonging to the delegates remained clean. The authorities declared their negotiation uncontaminated and attempted to resume it the following morning. Nobody could light the kitchen oven. Some blamed Maren's unfinished promise; others found that the assassin's accomplices had flooded the fuel store. The physical explanation did not settle the moral one.

The bowl's custody became a difficult honor. Its keeper was obliged to feed any person arriving with evidence of an interrupted negotiation, regardless of allegiance. During the Unannounced War, this included deserters, monopoly agents, and villagers whose homes both sides had burned. The vessel did not multiply food. Keeping its observance nearly bankrupted three successive households, and the fourth sold the house's roof beams to maintain the soup pot.

Its present keeper, Hessa, has installed the bowl in a cheap boarding kitchen near the river court. Visitors may inspect the repair but cannot touch it without first helping prepare a meal. Hessa rejects proposals for a grand memorial because admission fees would turn the obligation into a privilege purchased by spectators. She is less certain how to answer genuine starvation. The bowl's claim is finite—hospitality for a single night—but there are now more interrupted negotiations than the kitchen can accommodate. The sound of water dripping from its repaired rim persists even when the bowl is empty.
