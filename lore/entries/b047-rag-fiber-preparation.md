# Rag Fiber Preparation

*Trade · Vey · Historical coverage: 976–1248 AFR*

Workers sort cloth, wash fibers, and dry sheets slowly with finite fuel and space. Institutional patrons fund fine copies more readily. Public accounts credit this ordinary labor, making inquiry’s material infrastructure visible beside the ideas preserved upon it.

The trade depends upon tools, training, reliable materials, and people who perform work its public account barely mentions. A skilled craftsperson can improve a process, yet every batch still requires hands and a season of available materials. Output remains bounded by fuel, animals, weather, and the hands available to repair equipment. Prices carry the history of these constraints into households far from the workshop. A commercial undertaking that survives its maker can preserve useful cooperation or prevent successors from adapting to changed conditions. The present negotiation concerns how to retain knowledge while releasing an obligation nobody can now perform.

This belongs to The Games of the Unwritten Page. After an emergency regime ended, rival reform groups briefly governed together under a strict administrator whose private disciplines became public expectations. In 976 inquiry societies used demonstrations and handwritten exchanges to test claims once accepted through prestige alone. A cultural reader studied play as a serious source of shared rules rather than a childish distraction. A river battle involving shifting allies was remembered as the moment captive artisans introduced papermaking. Earlier fiber sheets complicated that convenient origin story. Schools learned to trace actual techniques through migration and exchange. Their surviving games and archives distinguish voluntary participation from witnessed undertakings, making room for experimentation without converting every agreed rule into an ancestral burden.

In the connected record, [The Discipline Without Consent](b047-the-discipline-without-consent.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Play and Oath Distinction](b047-the-play-and-oath-distinction.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: whether inquiry houses can keep access voluntary when patrons want their favored disciplines and origin stories treated as compulsory public truth.

Vey households measure wealth partly in dependable access to a public landing. Their markets exchange grain, lamp oil, glazed jars, and legal testimony. A witness can authenticate a transaction but cannot provide the labor needed to fulfill it. Seasonal crews clean the same embankments on which courts announce judgments, and a decision that ignores those crews soon meets an expensive physical limit. Flood deposits preserve small objects while destroying the arrangement that once made their meaning obvious. Recovering an object is therefore easier than establishing what anybody was entitled to do with it.
