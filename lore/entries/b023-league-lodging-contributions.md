# League Lodging Contributions

*Trade · Namar · Historical coverage: 1081–1248 AFR*

Contributors may offer food, repairs, or money, all recorded separately. Large donors cannot claim sole founding credit. Public accounts protect the lodge’s dependence on ordinary migrants whose small sustained labor often exceeds prestigious annual gifts.

The trade depends upon tools, training, reliable materials, and people who perform work its public account barely mentions. A skilled craftsperson can improve a process, yet every batch still requires hands and a season of available materials. Output remains bounded by fuel, animals, weather, and the hands available to repair equipment. Prices carry the history of these constraints into households far from the workshop. A commercial undertaking that survives its maker can preserve useful cooperation or prevent successors from adapting to changed conditions. The present negotiation concerns how to retain knowledge while releasing an obligation nobody can now perform.

This belongs to The League of the Dry Lake. A caravan kingdom prospered beside a lake whose channels gradually withdrew. After an envoy killed its ruler, patrons renamed the realm and redirected trade. Households dispersed across the basin but retained a league opposing inherited labor constraints. In 1081 a reformer formerly employed by an intrusive court joined a popular uprising and was later executed after promised safe passage failed. Traveling musicians changed styles repeatedly, frustrating demands for one authentic league sound. Rival ensembles attracted patron competition that sometimes overshadowed the shared purpose. The dried lake’s ruins remain a place where migration, solidarity, political compromise, and changing cultural practice are preserved without a single uncontested homeland story.

In the connected record, [Who Belongs to the Dry League](b023-who-belongs-to-the-dry-league.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Passage Promise Boundary](b023-the-passage-promise-boundary.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: whether the league can protect migrants who lack old lake ancestry without diluting the finite claims its founding households still need recognized.

The Namar passes join pasture settlements to caravan markets. Pack animals carry nearly everything that cannot be made from local stone, wool, or timber. Snow closes crossings irregularly, and a route that is short on a map may cost more food than a longer sheltered path. Hospices preserve undertakings made by travelers who never returned. Their custodians cannot assume that a visitor belongs to one sovereign or one witness tradition. A workable agreement must specify which season, which spring, and which people it protects, rather than claiming a universal welcome the stores cannot support.
