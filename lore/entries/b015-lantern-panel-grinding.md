# Lantern Panel Grinding

*Trade · Vey · Historical coverage: 1176–1248 AFR*

Workers grind panels by hand and reject pieces likely to crack near flame. Patrons demand colors beyond available minerals. The workshop refuses impossible quantities and records substitutions, making the theater’s splendor dependent on honest limitations rather than a fantasy of effortless illumination.

The trade depends upon tools, training, reliable materials, and people who perform work its public account barely mentions. A skilled craftsperson can improve a process, yet every batch still requires hands and a season of available materials. Output remains bounded by fuel, animals, weather, and the hands available to repair equipment. Prices carry the history of these constraints into households far from the workshop. A commercial undertaking that survives its maker can preserve useful cooperation or prevent successors from adapting to changed conditions. The present negotiation concerns how to retain knowledge while releasing an obligation nobody can now perform.

This belongs to The Theater of Linked Doors. After a long-serving magistrate withdrew in 1176, rival councils shared offices without agreeing who could issue orders. A traveling dramatist used colored lanterns and hinged scenery to stage judgments between living witnesses and dead claimants. Another performer, trained abroad, brought stories about inherited knowledge denied to younger relatives. Readers connected their manuscript copies through explicit page references, allowing an account in one town to point to evidence held in another. Patrols seized central catalogs but could not erase copies already dispersed. The network preserved testimony and also exposed vulnerable households when careless references revealed their locations. Its keepers now negotiate the difference between public evidence and information that enables coercion.

In the connected record, [The Shelter Reference Debate](b015-the-shelter-reference-debate.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Withheld Location Clause](b015-the-withheld-location-clause.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: which references should remain public when making testimony accessible can also identify people a patrol wants to seize.

Vey households measure wealth partly in dependable access to a public landing. Their markets exchange grain, lamp oil, glazed jars, and legal testimony. A witness can authenticate a transaction but cannot provide the labor needed to fulfill it. Seasonal crews clean the same embankments on which courts announce judgments, and a decision that ignores those crews soon meets an expensive physical limit. Flood deposits preserve small objects while destroying the arrangement that once made their meaning obvious. Recovering an object is therefore easier than establishing what anybody was entitled to do with it.
