# The Foundation Basket’s Dust

*Phenomenon · Vey · Historical coverage: 591–1248 AFR*

Fine dust returns inside the basket after each unmet drain assessment. Most matches exposed mortar nearby, but a covered test repeats the deposit. Custodians respond by recording unfinished work, linking the uncertainty to a task they already know requires completion.

Witnesses distinguish an observed effect from their explanation for it. Repetition under comparable conditions is valuable evidence; a striking story told after its alleged cause becomes famous is weaker evidence. Masons, gardeners, and boat crews often recognize patterns an interpreter overlooks. Their explanation may reduce the immediate danger without settling whether an undertaking contributed to it. The basin has no institution capable of issuing a final answer for every manifestation. Communities record the conditions under which the effect occurs, then negotiate a response whose costs they can actually sustain.

This belongs to The Capital Carried Downriver. Highland households fleeing famine joined beneath a negotiator who secured refuge through protection agreements rather than claiming an empty landscape. In 591 a dynasty moved its court downriver and founded a new capital beside the main grain landing. Its ceremonies announced victory before walls and drains were complete. A performer portraying a workshop organizer helped laborers compare the unequal terms under which they built the city. An appointed governor then joined distant dependencies into one jurisdiction to redirect unofficial trade. The new center gained real commercial strength while straining older refuge agreements. Present descendants occupy a layered street where royal splendor, migrant survival, and ordinary collective bargaining cannot be cleanly separated.

In the connected record, [Capital Stone Reuse](b022-capital-stone-reuse.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Protection Is Not Succession Clause](b022-the-protection-is-not-succession-clause.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: whether capital households can revise assessments without extinguishing the finite protection agreements under which highland refugees first settled.

Vey households measure wealth partly in dependable access to a public landing. Their markets exchange grain, lamp oil, glazed jars, and legal testimony. A witness can authenticate a transaction but cannot provide the labor needed to fulfill it. Seasonal crews clean the same embankments on which courts announce judgments, and a decision that ignores those crews soon meets an expensive physical limit. Flood deposits preserve small objects while destroying the arrangement that once made their meaning obvious. Recovering an object is therefore easier than establishing what anybody was entitled to do with it.
