# The Mask with the Wrong Fingerprint

*Current inquiry · Vey and Keth · Historical coverage: 1246–1248 AFR*

A reed mask purchased for a small collection contains a thin stone support where woven bark should have been. Its outer surface bears a household stamp used in Vey during the Unannounced War. Its inner surface preserves a fingertip impression associated with Keth’s later Cistern Assembly. The dates differ by more than half a century. A dealer advertised the object as proof that one hidden conspirator manipulated both events. Vesh bought it only after the dealer agreed to record that claim as unverified.

The mask almost certainly contains reused material. That does not explain whose hand touched the stone, why the stamp remained useful, or who later wore the altered mask. Vesh asked Nessa’s workshop for a preparation comparison, not a declaration of identity. Her successors found that the support had been dampened and cut long after the first impression formed. The object therefore traveled through at least two uses before entering the mask.

A related leaf in the Book of Unseated Names identifies an absent household whose signet was borrowed during Darr’s annexation assembly. The Vey stamp may have supplied an older assumed identity later reused by that household, or a broker may have sold it independently. Establishing either possibility requires the transfer accounts in the Heirs’ Drowned Room. The stone alone cannot decide.

Present reed cutters want the mask returned for their maintenance assembly, where face covering protects speakers but named tallies preserve responsibility. They reject the collection’s display title because it makes their anonymity look like proof of criminal secrecy. The owner fears losing a valuable exhibit. Vesh proposes a dry copy documenting the material layers and a finite loan for careful use, provided further wear will not destroy the impressions.

The inquiry has recovered no secret master of the basin. It has connected three systems of recognition whose gaps could help people escape coercion and also make them vulnerable to sale. In 1248, the most important pending question is a name beside an unexplained rent payment. Recovering its context could support an actual descendant’s claim. That modest outcome matters more than the dealer’s grand conspiracy, which supplies an entertaining explanation while erasing the separate lives the mask’s changing material may still help distinguish.
