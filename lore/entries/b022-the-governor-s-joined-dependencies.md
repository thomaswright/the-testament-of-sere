# The Governor’s Joined Dependencies

*Text · Vey · Historical coverage: 591–1248 AFR*

The jurisdiction lists distant places together without describing their different routes. Clerks apply one assessment to unequal journeys. Corrected route accounts expose why administrative unity cannot make every delivery cost the same amount of food and labor.

Copies disagree in ways that matter. A shortened phrase can remove a condition, and a copied date can imply that a later amendment belonged to the original undertaking. Dry writing preserves evidence but cannot make an oath binding by itself. Readers must distinguish a record of consent from a writer’s assertion that consent occurred. Marginal notes are sometimes the only surviving testimony of people excluded from the formal version. Establishing a trustworthy reading therefore depends on comparing custody, material, and ordinary expenses, as well as elegant arguments about wording.

This belongs to The Capital Carried Downriver. Highland households fleeing famine joined beneath a negotiator who secured refuge through protection agreements rather than claiming an empty landscape. In 591 a dynasty moved its court downriver and founded a new capital beside the main grain landing. Its ceremonies announced victory before walls and drains were complete. A performer portraying a workshop organizer helped laborers compare the unequal terms under which they built the city. An appointed governor then joined distant dependencies into one jurisdiction to redirect unofficial trade. The new center gained real commercial strength while straining older refuge agreements. Present descendants occupy a layered street where royal splendor, migrant survival, and ordinary collective bargaining cannot be cleanly separated.

In the connected record, [The Drain Arch Weaverbird](b022-the-drain-arch-weaverbird.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Protection Is Not Succession Clause](b022-the-protection-is-not-succession-clause.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: whether capital households can revise assessments without extinguishing the finite protection agreements under which highland refugees first settled.

Vey households measure wealth partly in dependable access to a public landing. Their markets exchange grain, lamp oil, glazed jars, and legal testimony. A witness can authenticate a transaction but cannot provide the labor needed to fulfill it. Seasonal crews clean the same embankments on which courts announce judgments, and a decision that ignores those crews soon meets an expensive physical limit. Flood deposits preserve small objects while destroying the arrangement that once made their meaning obvious. Recovering an object is therefore easier than establishing what anybody was entitled to do with it.
