# The Shelter Reference Debate

*Dispute · Vey · Historical coverage: 1176–1248 AFR*

A fugitive’s successful appeal required evidence that also revealed her host. Copyists want to publish the case; hosts request delayed locations. The dispute seeks a workable interval for accountability, recognizing that public truth can impose uneven dangers upon the people making it possible.

The dispute contains more than two positions. People with the same formal entitlement may face different practical costs, while opponents can depend upon the same canal, kitchen, or crossing. A complete settlement must describe an executable change rather than award a satisfying phrase to one side. Evidence about a past wrong remains necessary even when compensation cannot restore the original arrangement. Local negotiators separate immediate maintenance from the final assessment of responsibility. This allows a place to remain usable while its inhabitants continue an argument they have good reason not to abandon.

This belongs to The Theater of Linked Doors. After a long-serving magistrate withdrew in 1176, rival councils shared offices without agreeing who could issue orders. A traveling dramatist used colored lanterns and hinged scenery to stage judgments between living witnesses and dead claimants. Another performer, trained abroad, brought stories about inherited knowledge denied to younger relatives. Readers connected their manuscript copies through explicit page references, allowing an account in one town to point to evidence held in another. Patrols seized central catalogs but could not erase copies already dispersed. The network preserved testimony and also exposed vulnerable households when careless references revealed their locations. Its keepers now negotiate the difference between public evidence and information that enables coercion.

In the connected record, [Poven of the Hinged Judgment](b015-poven-of-the-hinged-judgment.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Withheld Location Clause](b015-the-withheld-location-clause.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: which references should remain public when making testimony accessible can also identify people a patrol wants to seize.

Vey households measure wealth partly in dependable access to a public landing. Their markets exchange grain, lamp oil, glazed jars, and legal testimony. A witness can authenticate a transaction but cannot provide the labor needed to fulfill it. Seasonal crews clean the same embankments on which courts announce judgments, and a decision that ignores those crews soon meets an expensive physical limit. Flood deposits preserve small objects while destroying the arrangement that once made their meaning obvious. Recovering an object is therefore easier than establishing what anybody was entitled to do with it.
