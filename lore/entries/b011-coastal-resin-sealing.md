# Coastal Resin Sealing

*Trade · Istren · Historical coverage: 423–1248 AFR*

Seal workers apply resin in small heated batches, using scarce fuel and careful ventilation. Export promises leave local hulls waiting. Their seasonal agreements prioritize safe working boats, linking commercial freedom to duties the exclusive grant originally claimed to support.

The trade depends upon tools, training, reliable materials, and people who perform work its public account barely mentions. A skilled craftsperson can improve a process, yet every batch still requires hands and a season of available materials. Output remains bounded by fuel, animals, weather, and the hands available to repair equipment. Prices carry the history of these constraints into households far from the workshop. A commercial undertaking that survives its maker can preserve useful cooperation or prevent successors from adapting to changed conditions. The present negotiation concerns how to retain knowledge while releasing an obligation nobody can now perform.

This belongs to The King Who Wrestled His Own Herald. King Varn reunited coastal districts after rival graves were desecrated in 423. He celebrated unity through wrestling exhibitions, casting his own herald as a rebellious villain whom the king repeatedly defeated. A promoter absorbed smaller fairs and controlled which local champions could appear. The court also granted an overseas house exclusive rights to gather waterproofing resin from royal forests. When raiders advanced along narrow tracks using light handcarts, defenders burned bridges but overlooked the paths known to resin workers. The royal spectacle continued while ordinary coastal households lost shelter. Present custodians preserve both unity’s genuine benefits and the neglected dependencies that made its claims vulnerable.

In the connected record, [The Forest and the Fair](b011-the-forest-and-the-fair.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Concession’s Boat Purpose](b011-the-concession-s-boat-purpose.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: whether forest households can renegotiate the resin concession without forfeiting the boat repairs their coast still needs.

Istren’s fishing villages and timber monasteries occupy a rainy coast where repairing roofs is a continuing expense rather than a finished achievement. Boats make neighboring settlements accessible when inland tracks become mud. Smokehouses, net yards, and communal ovens provide the places where people exchange reliable news. The dead are remembered through finite promises attached to landing water and particular working seasons. A claim on an abandoned beach can become dangerous when erosion moves a new household into its reach. Islanders generally test an alleged manifestation against wind, rot, and tides before paying for an interpreter.
