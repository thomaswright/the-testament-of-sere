# Peace Garden Cultivation

*Trade · Suth · Historical coverage: 779–1248 AFR*

Gardeners schedule harvest around talks while reserving time for pruning and channel work. Sponsors fund impressive meeting tents more readily. Public accounts make maintenance visible, keeping peace’s setting alive rather than sacrificing cultivation to its ceremonial image.

The trade depends upon tools, training, reliable materials, and people who perform work its public account barely mentions. A skilled craftsperson can improve a process, yet every batch still requires hands and a season of available materials. Output remains bounded by fuel, animals, weather, and the hands available to repair equipment. Prices carry the history of these constraints into households far from the workshop. A commercial undertaking that survives its maker can preserve useful cooperation or prevent successors from adapting to changed conditions. The present negotiation concerns how to retain knowledge while releasing an obligation nobody can now perform.

This belongs to The Epic and the Unoccupied Garden. An upland founder’s life survived through performed epic alongside sparse independent records. Later households used the epic to justify incompatible claims to a fertile valley. In 779 a negotiator established a garden zone from which armed officers withdrew for talks, hoping protected meeting space would end recurring conflict. Outside coalitions continued contesting its borders and access. A naval battle far downstream redirected trade, weakening sponsors who had supplied the negotiations and strengthening new patrons. The peace zone became both a genuine refuge and a place where rivals accumulated influence. Present gardeners compare heroic memory, signed terms, and changed supply routes to preserve talks without pretending withdrawal of one force made the valley free of coercion.

In the connected record, [The Garden’s New Sponsor](b039-the-garden-s-new-sponsor.md) preserves another part of this history. The terms recorded under [The Resident Within Neutrality](b039-the-resident-within-neutrality.md) delimit the local obligation; they do not grant a ruler unlimited command over the dead. The unresolved issue in 1248 is concrete: whether the meeting garden can remain protected when the trade revenues supporting its food and repairs now pass through rival downstream hands.

The Suth valleys cultivate grafted orchards and irrigated gardens amid dry hills. Food production depends on precise local knowledge: when to open a channel, which rootstock tolerates mineral water, and how many hands a harvest requires. Asylum customs let strangers work under temporary names, but temporary recognition does not automatically convey inheritance. The same gardens can preserve an old promise and expose its injustice. A court that assigns water without accounting for the labor of keeping it clean may issue a valid judgment that no household can afford to obey.
