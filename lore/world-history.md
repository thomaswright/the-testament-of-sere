# A history of things carried

Sere's history is easiest to follow through objects that outlast the authority under which they were first used: a cup, a weaving pattern, a key, a song, a damaged account. No dynasty has governed the whole basin continuously. Institutions become powerful when they preserve useful cooperation across a change of ruler, then dangerous when they mistake that usefulness for an unlimited claim.

## A common count, divided shores

The [First Reckoning](entries/the-first-reckoning.md) gave witness cities a calendar with which to compare obligations after a ruler's death. It supplied neither a universal court nor a creation story. The [Council of Faceted Water](sources/batch-041.md), founded in 89, extended cooperation through crossings, messenger routes, and famine stores. Its limits matter throughout later history: maintaining a route did not entitle the council to govern every household using it.

The sea king's union ended in 156. Three crowns had shared one ruler while retaining separate laws; his death exposed the difference. Later persecution on the named coast scattered households whose schools preserved places and people through [progressive lessons](entries/b030-the-progressive-lesson-houses.md). Their music carried memory farther than the vanished royal office could carry jurisdiction. Neither beautiful performance nor sincere grief, however, established the exact responsibility for each lost home.

## An empire survives in work

Keth's shelf empire dissolved in 349, leaving no simple empty age. Weaving households carried techniques, prayers, and credentials into successor settlements. The [Thread Houses](sources/batch-016.md) became evidence claimed by rival courts, while practical teaching continued beneath those incompatible claims. The distinction between cultural continuity and political succession became a recurring defense against annexation.

One city retained an officer and provisioning offices after the empire fell. Its conquest in 402 placed a new ruler's ancient name above institutions that had never stopped feeding people. The [Last Officer's history](sources/batch-036.md) therefore resists a clean story of destruction followed by restoration. Later occupations divided the same city while weaving schools carried knowledge across its wall. On the burial shelves, [competing successor lists](entries/the-terraces-of-the-second-foot.md) made this political ambiguity bodily dangerous.

## Refuge becomes a capital

Highland famine households gained refuge through agreements with people already occupying the river land. When a dynasty moved its court downriver in 591, that landing became a capital before its drains were complete. The [new capital](sources/batch-022.md) depended upon migrant survival, builders' cooperation, and grain carried along older routes. Royal recognition could protect newcomers while also threatening the local terms that had first admitted them.

The old inland temple accumulated foundations and competing recitations as the river deposited silt. In 903, the city confined residents suspected of coastal ancestry; neighboring households acquired their workshops. The [Separate Restitution Clause](entries/b004-the-separate-restitution-clause.md) later distinguished a changed profession of belief from repair for an actual confiscation. The deeper foundation and the displaced household are parts of the same city history. Its sacred origin does not place its later conduct beyond inquiry.

## Distance and fragile abundance

During the repair centuries, techniques, stories, and cargo crossed jurisdictions that could not agree about inheritance. Classifying stars became both useful observation and an excuse to classify people. Fragrant bark reached western warehouses through routes whose source names traders sometimes concealed. Food and craft circulated with the obligations attached to their production. The [cargo disturbance](entries/the-cargo-that-arrived-as-a-forest.md) records one costly failure to distinguish possession of an object from unrestricted control of its history.

The White Reach expedition returned in 1197, its achievement inseparable from stored provisions, depots, and borrowed local knowledge. Its [crew consent rule](entries/b044-the-journey-s-informed-crew-term.md) belongs to a longer argument about whether sponsorship purchases obedience. The drowned chamber exploration in 1221 raised a related question: reaching old masonry could supply observations without validating a successor court's claim to an ancient capital.

The mint-quarter disaster of 1232 broke houses, stores, and evidence together. The [Available Goods Relief Test](entries/b048-the-available-goods-relief-test.md) confronted the gap between recorded wealth and food someone could actually receive. In 1248 that gap persists across the basin. The [Blue Tile Convoy](entries/the-blue-tile-convoy.md), [Peace Garden negotiations](entries/the-last-season-at-the-peace-garden.md), and [orchard tenancy](entries/the-orchard-between-two-deeds.md) connect old practices through present deliveries and costs. History remains active because people still carry its water, repair its landings, and argue over whose names its useful objects are permitted to retain.
